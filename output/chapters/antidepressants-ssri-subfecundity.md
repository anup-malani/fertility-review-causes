# Chapter: Antidepressants and Pharmacological Subfecundity

**Category:** Biological
**Primary mechanism:** Antidepressant medication impairs sexual desire and orgasm in a substantial share of users and may perturb the reproductive endocrine axis, so a population in which a large fraction of reproductive-age adults is medicated conceives at a lower rate than it otherwise would, without anyone's intentions having changed.
**Target phenomena:** The second demographic transition only, and within it only the period after 1988. No pre-modern cell and no first-transition cell: the exposure did not exist.
**Cross-references:** coital frequency and fecundability (A.14, which owns the step from behaviour to conception and whose parameter is what shrinks this hypothesis); the mental-health epidemic (D.3.a, which owns the condition being treated and whose measured effect is larger than this one's); fetal loss (B.5, whose channel the antidepressant-and-miscarriage literature belongs to); endocrine disruptors and microplastics (B.2, B.6, the same endocrine pathway reached by environmental rather than pharmacological exposure); paternal age and sperm quality (A.16, which shares this chapter's male-side outcomes).
**Draft status:** DRAFT. The reconnaissance, search, screen, and demographic-significance computation are complete and reproducible. Retrieval is **partial**: 6 of the 20 primary-cell records were obtainable through an open route and 3 are extracted. The RA gate worksheet is generated and unsigned. The GRADE ratings were produced by one analyst applying three lenses rather than by three independent raters, which is a stated deviation from PROTOCOL §5.11. Five scope calls are open with the PI.

## 1. Verdict

Antidepressants demonstrably impair sexual function, prevalence has risen a great deal, and the inference from those two facts to a population fertility effect is where the hypothesis fails. It fails at a specific joint, for a reason that is measurable, and the failure is not for want of looking.

| Phenomenon | Causal credibility | Demographic significance | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-modern | Not applicable | Not applicable | The exposure did not exist. |
| First transition | Not applicable | Not applicable | Tricyclics date from the 1950s but prescribing volumes were negligible and the period closes in 1965. |
| Second transition | **Very low** | **Not significant**: 0.08% of the post-1988 OECD TFR decline; 3.4% at the no-recuperation upper bound | The chapter's entire quantitative claim rests on one confidence interval, from one cohort, on one sex, and it includes the null. |

Five findings drive these ratings.

**Two thirds of the decline this hypothesis is assigned to explain was over before its cause existed.** Fluoxetine reached the US market in 1988. OECD total fertility had already fallen from 3.13 in 1965 to 2.01 by then, which is 67.6% of the total decline to 2024. B.7 cannot have caused any of that. Scored against the full second-transition denominator as PROTOCOL §4.2 is currently written, the hypothesis is credited with variation that predates its own existence; scored against the post-1988 change, it faces a smaller and much more demanding denominator. This chapter uses the post-1988 figure and reports the other alongside. The problem generalises to every late-arriving exposure in this review, which is why it is raised as a call rather than settled here.

**The hypothesis is a chain of three links, and the evidence is piled almost entirely on the first.** Antidepressant use impairs sexual function; impaired sexual function reduces coital frequency; reduced coital frequency reduces conception. Of 420 records screened from the citation frame, 71 speak to the first link, 12 to the third, and **one** to the second — a qualitative interview study of nine women, held in a university repository. The step the hypothesis actually needs is the one nobody has measured.

**The third link argues against the hypothesis rather than for it.** Conception probability per cycle is close to flat above two or three well-timed acts of intercourse, and for couples trying to conceive the binding behavioural factor is *timing*, not frequency. A desire decrement therefore has to be very large before it moves the conception hazard at all in the population that is trying. Where B.7 could still bite is on people who are not deliberately trying — whether a partnership forms, persists, or produces an unplanned conception — and that is a different mechanism from the one the hypothesis states, with no evidence at all behind it.

**One study in the whole frame estimates a fertility outcome against antidepressant exposure while adjusting for the indication, and its interval includes the null.** Yland and colleagues' 2022 analysis of 2,398 men in a North American preconception cohort reports a fecundability ratio of 0.85 with a 95% interval of [0.65, 1.12] for current SSRI use, and 1.03 [0.71, 1.48] for other antidepressants. Restricting to the incident attempt period attenuates the SSRI estimate to 0.91 [0.66, 1.26]. The paper states in its own introduction that no studies had evaluated the relationship between psychotropic medication use and directly measured fertility outcomes — an independent confirmation, from inside the literature, of what the screen found from outside it.

**The condition being treated has a larger effect on fertility than the medication, and it is measured far better.** In the Norwegian population register, depression through the reproductive period corresponds to completed fertility of 1.34 births per woman against 1.60 for women with none of the disorders studied, and 0.90 against 1.41 for men. B.7's claim is that the drug adds a decrement on top of that. The only estimate of the addition is the interval above.

## 2. The Claim and the Mechanism

The claim is pharmacological and behavioural at once. SSRIs block serotonin reuptake; elevated serotonin damps the dopaminergic reward circuitry that drives sexual motivation and interferes with the autonomic processes underlying orgasm. These effects are not in dispute. What is in dispute is whether they aggregate into a fertility effect.

B.7's structural difficulty is the mirror image of B.5's. B.5 was defined by a *channel* whose drivers all belonged to other chapters. B.7 is defined by a *treatment* — a specific, precisely dated, well-measured pharmaceutical exposure — whose *outcome* belongs to another chapter. The medication's proximate effect is on sexual function; sexual function's effect on conception is A.14's; and the fertility quantity this review cares about sits two links further down. What B.7 owns is the drug and the first link. Everything beyond that is inference, and the chapter's job is to price each step rather than to assert the product.

### The chain, and where it breaks

| Link | Proposition | Records located | Best evidence | Rating |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Antidepressant use → sexual dysfunction | 71 | Double-blind placebo-controlled fixed-dose trials on ejaculation; a prospective multicentre incidence study of 1,022 outpatients using direct questioning | **Moderate** |
| 2 | Sexual dysfunction → reduced coital frequency | **1** | An interpretive-phenomenological interview study of nine women, plus one mediation proportion (17%) computed for depression rather than for medication | **Very low** |
| 3 | Reduced coital frequency → reduced conception | 12 | Day-specific conception probabilities; joint models of intercourse behaviour and fecundability; the finding that mistiming rather than frequency is the primary behavioural cause of failure to conceive | **Moderate — and it cuts against the claim** |

A chain is no stronger than its weakest link, and the composite claim therefore rates far below link 1. This matters practically: a chapter that rated the evidence it *has* rather than the evidence the claim *needs* would return a confident rating for a proposition nobody disputes and would tell a reader nothing about fertility. The per-link table is reported so the reader can see exactly where the argument stops.

### Exposure duration is the deflator, and it is large

The second quantitative problem is the direct analogue of B.5's, arriving from the opposite direction. Write $p$ for the share of reproductive-age person-time spent under exposure and $\delta$ for the proportional reduction in the conception hazard while exposed. The product $p\delta$ is the share of conceptions displaced during exposure — but a conception not achieved this month is not a birth forgone. It is a birth **postponed**, and where the reproductive span does not bind, it is recovered after discontinuation.

At the central estimate, a current SSRI user's per-cycle conception probability falls from 0.20 to 0.17, which lengthens the mean wait to conception by **0.88 months per attempt**. Two attempts cost about 1.8 months of a reproductive span running to roughly 25 years. That delay converts into a lost birth only for women whose remaining slack is shorter than it. Every estimate in this chapter therefore carries an estimand level:

| Level | What it measures |
|---|---|
| `HAZARD_DECREMENT` | The effect on the per-cycle conception hazard or on time-to-pregnancy. An input, not a fertility quantity, and an upper bound reported only as one. |
| `TEMPO_ADJUSTED_QUANTUM` | The effect on completed fertility after whatever recuperation the population actually does. The quantity the demographic-significance verdict requires. |

The two are never pooled. There is also a channel that escapes the deflator entirely: exposure that changes whether a union forms or persists removes births rather than postponing them. That channel is not deflated by recuperation, and it is the one for which the chapter has no evidence whatever.

### The sign is not established

Antidepressants impair sexual function and they also treat a condition that impairs fertility through several channels. The net effect of medicating an indicated person could run either way, and v5's claim text assumes the pharmacological decrement dominates without argument. The screen turned up records pointing the other way: sexual function improving as depressive symptoms fall under escitalopram; psychiatric intervention raising pregnancy rates in infertile couples; imipramine used to restore antegrade ejaculation; paroxetine improving sperm quality in a depressed animal model; and, in the one identified human estimate, a point estimate above 1 for non-SSRI antidepressants. A chapter that reported a uniform decrement would be reporting something the evidence does not contain.

## 3. Search Strategy

Reproducible from `source/build/goldset/123_b7_recon_probe.py` through `131_b7_extract_retrieved.py`. The full specification, including eight boundary walls and the estimand taxonomy, is in `literature/search-logs/antidepressants-ssri-subfecundity-search-scope.md`, written and frozen before the search ran.

A 54-probe reconnaissance pass over OpenAlex (2026-08-12, zero failed requests) established the shape of the problem in advance. Antidepressants paired with pregnancy return roughly 5,650 records; paired with any fertility-rate term, 48 — and every one of the most-cited among those 48 is a pregnancy-safety study that happens to use the phrase "birth rate". The seam is not thin; it is close to absent. Probes for aggregate or country-level antidepressant-and-births work returned 35 records with nothing on topic.

Twenty-four cold-start anchors were sourced live and put through four gates: existence, version-of-record, book-canon, and a **shadow-record gate** built for this chapter. A shadow record is a real, separately-indexed work whose title is the target title plus a leading qualifier — `Editorial Comment to X`, `Faculty Opinions recommendation of X`, `Re: X`, `Expression of Concern: X` — and it defeats the other three gates by construction. Five fired here. The sharpest case: neither index copy of the 1,022-patient incidence study carries a DOI, while the Faculty Opinions comment on it does, so a DOI-preferring resolver without this gate would anchor the study to a one-paragraph post-publication note. One shadow was an Expression of Concern on a highly-cited mechanism study, and it is carried forward as an integrity flag rather than discarded.

Twenty-two verified anchors seeded a one-hop citation frame of 7,174 records, deduplicated to 6,798. Forward citation was applied uniformly across seed types including routing decoys, with seed provenance retained. A deterministic two-axis rank produced a 420-record worklist, screened against a 25-cell routing rubric. The residual is characterised rather than implied: 6,378 records went unscreened, of which 5,457 carry no fertility-axis term at all.

**The on-topic diagnostic is the chapter's thesis stated as a number.** The share of each seed's citation cloud carrying any fertility quantity runs 51–68% for the primary and mechanism seeds and **0.7–2.1% for the link-1 seeds**. The enormous literature establishing that antidepressants impair sexual function almost never touches fertility.

**Every retrieved estimate is Serious or Critical on ROBINS-I, and the binding domain is the same one in all of them.** Nobody has an active comparator, a within-person contrast, or an instrument for prescribing, so the medication cannot be separated from the reason it was prescribed. This is not a criticism of any individual study; it is the state of the literature.

One further point about study value here. A large, well-powered registry study of medicated versus unmedicated women, adjusted for age, income, and education, is *not* informative about B.7 no matter how large it is, because the adjustment set does not contain the reason for the prescription. Sample size is close to uninformative about study worth in this chapter, and the extraction and risk-of-bias stages are built to say so.

## 6. Quantitative Synthesis

**No pooling.** PROTOCOL §5.9 directs meta-analysis at three or more studies with extractable effect sizes, and the count is met only by counting rows rather than studies. The rows are not commensurable: a fecundability ratio, a set of semen-parameter p-values, and a pharmacovigilance signal share no estimand, no outcome scale, and no population. A pooled figure assembled from them would have no referent. Narrative synthesis, with the single identified estimate reported as such and a decomposition built on parameters with an explicit uncertainty range in §7.

The publication-bias diagnostics PROTOCOL §6 asks for cannot be computed on one estimate. What can be said is qualitative and points one way: the positive semen-parameter findings come from small clinical series, the largest null the search located sits in a general urology journal, and the one systematically-collected fertility estimate has an interval spanning the null.

### 7.1 Pre-modern and 7.2 First transition

### 7.3 Second transition

The timing wall first, because it bounds everything after it.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| OECD TFR, 1965 | 3.134 |
| OECD TFR, 1988 (market entry) | 2.014 |
| OECD TFR, 2024 | 1.478 |
| Total decline | 1.656 births (52.9%) |
| **Share complete before the exposure existed** | **67.6%** |
| Decline available to B.7 | 0.537 births (26.7%) |

Against the post-1988 denominator, at central parameters — 6.0% of reproductive-age person-time exposed, FR = 0.85, and a recuperation deflator of τ = 0.024 derived from a slack model rather than assumed:

| Level | Share of births | Share of the post-1988 decline | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `HAZARD_DECREMENT` (no recuperation) | 0.90% of conceptions | 3.4% | not significant |
| `TEMPO_ADJUSTED_QUANTUM` (the verdict) | 0.02% of births | **0.08%** | **not significant** |

**The most favourable reading, computed rather than dismissed.** A negative verdict is worth nothing unless the corner of the parameter space that most favours the hypothesis has been calculated and stated. Taking the highest plausible exposure prevalence (13%), the strong end of a confidence interval that includes the null (FR = 0.65), and assuming a delayed conception is simply a lost birth, the share reaches **17.1%** — above PROTOCOL §4.2's threshold. The hypothesis therefore crosses into significance only if all three of those are granted at once. The chapter's argument against the third is the same argument B.5 makes against reading $(1-p)$ as an effect on completed fertility, and it does not depend on the other two.

**What the computation cannot do.** The effect size is one interval from one cohort of 2,398 men, and it includes the null. The arithmetic performed on it is robust; the input is not. No female-side fecundability estimate adjusted for the indication was located anywhere in the frame, so the parameter carrying this entire computation is male and is being applied to a population effect the hypothesis locates in women. Slope sufficiency is therefore "insufficient" and the conditional R² benchmark is not computable: there is no panel of antidepressant prevalence against TFR to run it on, because nobody has built one.

## 8. GRADE Rating

Assigned to the **composite claim** — that antidepressant exposure reduces fertility — with the three links rated separately so a reader can see where the chain breaks. Rating link 1 and reporting that rating would credit the hypothesis with the quality of evidence for a proposition it does not need to establish.

| Target | Rating | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| Link 1: medication → sexual function | Moderate | Randomised, placebo-controlled, fixed-dose evidence exists, replicated across agents and settings. Held below High by ascertainment: measured incidence varies several-fold between spontaneous report and structured questioning, and the two are not pooled. |
| Link 2: sexual function → coital frequency | Very low | One qualitative interview study of nine women. One mediation proportion, computed for depression rather than for medication. |
| Link 3: coital frequency → conception | Moderate | Well-studied, formally modelled, and it establishes a strong non-linearity that works against the hypothesis. Owned by A.14; earns no credit here. |
| **Composite: antidepressants → fertility** | **Very low** | One prospective cohort with controls but no identification of the indication; not replicated; interval includes the null; the point estimate for non-SSRI antidepressants sits above 1. Under PROTOCOL §4.1 a single credible observational study without replication would be "Low"; the inconsistent evidence pattern and the unmeasured second link pull it to Very low. A rater could defensibly say Low, and the disagreement is recorded rather than resolved. |

**Deviation from protocol.** PROTOCOL §5.11 requires three independent agent raters with escalation to the PI on disagreements greater than one level. These ratings were produced by one analyst applying three lenses. This is a stated deviation and the panel should be run before sign-off.

## 9. Verdict

| | Causal credibility | Demographic significance |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-modern | Not applicable | Not applicable |
| First transition | Not applicable | Not applicable |
| Second transition | Very low | Not significant (0.08%; 3.4% at the no-recuperation bound; 17.1% only at the most favourable corner) |

The honest summary is narrower than either the hypothesis or its critics would like. Antidepressants impair sexual function — that much is well established. Whether that impairment moves the number of children a population has is not known, because the step from sexual function to sexual behaviour has not been measured in this population, the step from behaviour to conception is known to be weakly binding among couples who are trying, and the one study that estimates the whole path at once cannot separate the drug from the depression. On the arithmetic that can be done, the effect is small enough to be demographically negligible unless several unfavourable assumptions are granted together. **This is a chapter about an absence of evidence, and the absence is specific enough to be worth reporting as a finding.**

## 10. Open Questions and Recommended Studies

**What would change the verdict.**

1. **A within-person or active-comparator design with a fertility outcome.** The single most valuable study would exploit variation in prescribing that is not generated by the patient's own reproductive circumstances: formulary changes, reimbursement discontinuities, prescriber-preference instruments, guideline changes, patent expiry. Nordic prescription registers linked to birth registers can support this and have been used for exactly this design on other outcomes — the paternal-SSRI cohort in this chapter's own held set does it, on offspring outcomes rather than on conception. The method exists; it has not been pointed at this question.
2. **Anything at all on link 2.** A cohort measuring antidepressant exposure, sexual function, and coital frequency together would price the joint the hypothesis rests on. One qualitative study of nine women is what the literature currently offers.
3. **A female-side fecundability estimate adjusted for the indication.** The AJOG companion to this chapter's central study exists and is not openly available; obtaining it is a library task rather than a research one, and it would double the identified evidence base.
4. **Exposure prevalence by age and sex over time, on a consistent basis.** The demographic-significance computation is more sensitive to this than to the effect size at present, and the best series is paywalled.

**What would not change it.** More link-1 studies. There are seventy-one already and the proposition is not in dispute.

**Open scope calls with the PI.** (1) Whether to score late-arriving exposures against a post-entry denominator, which generalises to B.6, C.2.h, D.3.b and C.3.b and probably belongs in PROTOCOL §4.2 rather than in this chapter. (2) Whether the verdict scores the net effect of medication against the pharmacological decrement alone. (3) Whether B.7 should be widened from antidepressants to psychotropics, given that antipsychotic-induced hyperprolactinaemia is a mechanistically stronger fecundity pathway at lower prevalence. (4) Whether GRADE attaches to the chain or to link 1. (5) Two corrections to the v5 entry: the male-fertility review cited as "Beeder and Bhatt (2025)" is Beeder and Samplaski, *International Journal of Urology*, 2019, and the claim text locates the mechanism in women while the measured fertility evidence is male.

**A data-integrity note carried forward.** One of the most-cited mechanism studies in this literature — a 2008 report of sperm DNA damage under SSRI treatment — carries a 2023 Expression of Concern in the same journal. The flag was raised automatically by the shadow-record gate and is recorded in `literature/search-logs/antidepressants-ssri-subfecundity-cold-start-anchors-log.md`. It must be read before that study's effect size is used anywhere.
