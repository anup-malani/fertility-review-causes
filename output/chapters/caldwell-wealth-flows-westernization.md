**Category:** Cultural

## 2. The Claim and Mechanism

The claim is that a belief can cause the thing it describes. A package of ideas about what it means to be modern — that a modern family is nuclear and conjugal rather than extended and kin-embedded, that it marries late by choice rather than early by arrangement, that spouses are equals, that children are few and heavily invested in — has spread globally from Western societies through mass schooling, mass media, development agencies, and direct contact. Where the package is absorbed, family aspirations move toward it, and fertility follows.

What gives the hypothesis content is the counterfactual attached to it. The claim is not that culture matters, which no one disputes, but that **the belief travels ahead of the structural change it forecasts and can act without it.** People come to believe that small families are what modern people have before the income growth, urbanization, mortality decline, or child-cost inflation that would make small families advantageous has arrived. If that is right, fertility can fall in places where the standard economic preconditions have not been met.

Two formulations sit behind this. Caldwell routed the mechanism through mass schooling, arguing that a school restructures authority and obligation inside a household — relocating a child's day, deference, and future orientation away from the family economy — before it changes any wage or price. Thornton's developmental idealism is the general form: ordinary people hold a causal *theory* about development, propagated by states and international organizations, and acting on that theory is itself a cause of demographic change.

The hypothesis is therefore defined against its own neighbours, and the definition is the hard part. Every structural theory of fertility decline predicts the same correlation between exposure to modernity and low fertility, because Western contact, schooling, media access, urbanization, income, and mortality decline all arrive together. A raw association between modern ideals and small families is consistent with the entire literature. Section 4 sets out the six boundaries this chapter is drawn against and the one cell that can distinguish it.

## 3. How the Evidence Is Organized

Two distinctions structure everything downstream, and both are enforced in the extraction schema rather than left to prose.

**The outcome level.** The developmental-idealism package is a claim about the whole family form, so its earliest observable effects appear in marriage and residence rather than in births.

| Outcome level | What it measures | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Realized fertility | Births, parity, completed family size | The decisive stratum |
| Stated intention or ideal | Intentions, desired or ideal family size | In scope, always flagged as stated |
| Family-formation behaviour | Age at marriage, spouse choice, marriage arrangement, nuclear residence | In scope as mechanism evidence |

**The three levels are never pooled**, and the assembler emits three separate files precisely so that no later step can combine them. A marriage-timing result is evidence that the package is being absorbed. It is not evidence about fertility until a birth is counted.

**Whether structural change is held fixed.** Every included paper records which of household income or wealth, urbanization, child mortality, women's employment, schooling-as-human-capital, and contraceptive access its design actually adjusts for. This is not descriptive metadata. The hypothesis's distinctive claim is independence from structural change, so an estimate's identification value is set entirely by this field. An association between developmental-idealism beliefs and low fertility that adjusts for none of them documents a correlation and cannot be described as evidence that belief moved fertility on its own.

## 4. Inclusion Approach

The review began with 28 independently chosen anchor studies spanning the four transmission channels — measured developmental-idealism beliefs, schooling as an ideational carrier, media carrying a modern-family model, and direct Western contact — together with the theory canon and eight routing decoys, one for each boundary this chapter is drawn against. The anchors seeded a candidate pool built from the studies they cite and the studies that cite them, reaching papers by a route that does not inherit a keyword search's blind spots.

**Six boundary walls, frozen before screening.** D.1.b sits at the intersection of the most heavily populated literatures in the review, so almost every candidate belongs to a neighbour and routing is the central screening task. A paper leaves this chapter if its operative variable is an intergenerational *transfer* (C.3.f, which is the other half of Caldwell and shares his vocabulary wholesale); a *self*-oriented value shift inside an already-rich society (D.1.a); the *geometry* of a diffusion channel rather than its content (A.20); *contraceptive* knowledge or legitimation as the thing that spreads (A.3); a woman's *own* autonomy or bargaining position (D.2.a); the *cost or return* to schooling rather than what schooling teaches (C.3.b, C.2.e); or a formally modelled *prestige-biased* copying process (D.1.c). In each case the rule is the same: judge the estimand, not the framing sentence. A study measuring net transfers routes to C.3.f even when it cites Caldwell's cultural argument.

**Two scope decisions were taken deliberately and are recorded because both narrow the chapter.**

*The first transition cell covers the diffused transition only.* For the historical Western transition of roughly 1870–1930 the westernization claim is degenerate — there was no external West to import a family model from, and the within-Europe spread of small-family practice along linguistic and religious lines is the Princeton finding, which this review files at A.3 and A.20. Carrying it would make this chapter a near-duplicate of A.3. The historical Western transition therefore enters only as the source of the package.

*Reduced-form schooling estimates stay out of the pool.* Most schooling→fertility estimates decompose no mechanism at all, and are therefore neither this chapter's hypothesis nor C.3.b's on their own evidence. They are recorded under `MECHANISM_UNRESOLVED_SCHOOLING`, reported as a count, and never pooled. Admitting them would let the best-identified literature in the review answer a question it was not designed to answer.

**A limit of the screen, stated in advance rather than discovered in an audit.** A title/abstract screen cannot enforce the schooling wall. Whether a schooling estimate separates ideational content from wage returns lives in the results tables, invisible to an abstract. The same limit defeated the corresponding wall in the companion chapter on climate anxiety. The wall is therefore enforced at extraction, and the screen's unresolved-schooling count should be read as an upper bound on how much of that literature is genuinely unassignable.

## 11. Interpretation for Economics Readers

The hypothesis proposes a preference change with an external cause and no accompanying price change. In the standard model, fertility falls when the relative price of children rises or income effects shift demand. Here the claim is that a belief about what modern life looks like enters the utility function directly, arriving from outside the economy rather than from within it, and moves desired family size before any budget constraint has moved.

That framing makes the identification problem legible. The exposures the hypothesis names — schooling, media, Western contact, development programmes — are exactly the variables every structural theory also uses, and they arrive as a bundle. So the empirical content of this chapter rests on a narrow class of designs: those that hold structural change fixed while ideational exposure varies, or that find settings where ideation moved and structure did not. That class is `DIFFUSION_INDEPENDENT_OF_STRUCTURE`, and it is expected to be small.

It is worth naming in advance that the anchor set deliberately includes a study that races cultural transmission against economic models on the same data and finds for the economic ones. A cell defined by *design* must contain the designs that come out against the hypothesis, or it selects on its own conclusion.

## 13. Reproducibility Appendix

**Stages completed and committed.**

Twenty-eight existence-verified anchors: 12 empirical across four channels, 6 theory canon, 8 routing decoys covering every wall plus the first-transition restriction, 1 contrary-evidence design, 1 dual-homed media case. Every DOI was pulled from a live Crossref or OpenAlex match and re-affirmed at doi.org; none was asserted from memory.

The citation frame reached **4,701 deduplicated candidates** from those anchors — 3,123 with usable abstracts, 44 found by both the backward and forward channels, none deferred. Tier A holds 14 empirical seeds populating all five cells. The blinded screen covers the 3,123 abstract-bearing records in 125 batches of 25; the remaining 1,578 title-only records were assigned `UNCERTAIN` / `INSUFFICIENT_INFO` by the rubric's own title-only policy without a model call, and they remain in the corpus and in every denominator.

**The screen is paused at 65 of 125 batches** (1,625 of 3,123 abstract-bearing records). A partial assembly exists at `output/caldwell-wealth-flows-westernization-PARTIAL-*` and every one of its files carries the partial marker in its filename and its header. Batch assignment was a deterministic shuffle, so the screened half is a random sample and its rates are unbiased estimates of the whole — which is exactly why nothing from it is quoted in this chapter. No count from the partial assembly is a finding, and none is used as a denominator here.

**Two method faults were found and fixed in this chapter's build, both recorded because they affect how much the numbers above can be trusted.**

The mandatory existence-verification gate — no anchor without a live DOI — passed 8 of 28 anchors at the *wrong version* of the right paper: working papers for two of the media quasi-experiments, a preprint, a 2024 reprint, and a book review standing in for Thornton's 2005 monograph. All passed at title similarity 1.0, because a preprint's title is identical to the article's. The gate catches titles that resolve to nothing; it cannot catch titles that resolve to the wrong real thing. Anchoring on a working paper would have shrunk the Tier B recall yardstick along with its own denominator, leaving the recall figure looking correct. The resolver now ranks candidates for version-of-record status, and the same rule was subsequently found missing at the *next* stage, where it had to be applied again.

The screen validator, inherited from a chapter whose taxonomy had no sibling routers, initially rejected 22 of 40 correct verdicts on its first live batch by insisting that a not-relevant paper carry no routing cell. In this taxonomy the verdict says whether a paper belongs here and the cell says where it does belong, so `NOT_RELEVANT` with a routing label is the correct and expected disposition. A fail-closed validator that is itself wrong does not merely miss errors; it manufactures them and discards good work.
