# Chapter: Cultural Westernization and Developmental Idealism

**Category:** Cultural
**Primary mechanism:** A belief about what modern families look like — nuclear, late-marrying, egalitarian, investing heavily in few children — diffuses from Western societies through schooling, media, and development institutions, and reshapes family aspirations before, or without, the economic change the belief describes.
**Target phenomena:** First transition (the *diffused* transition only — post-1945 societies entering transition under exposure to an external model) and second transition. There is no pre-modern cell.
**Cross-references:** intergenerational wealth flows (C.3.f, the economic mechanism this cultural shift is claimed to produce); postmaterialist value change (D.1.a); diffusion channels and diffusion of fertility control (A.20, A.3); female empowerment (D.2.a); compulsory schooling (C.3.b); cultural evolution (D.1.c); intergenerational transmission (A.19).
**Draft status:** **INTERIM — retrieval-bound, and the evidence base is three studies.** The screen is 54% complete (67 of 125 batches) and open-access retrieval reached 17 of 88 priority records (19%). Those 17 have been read at full text and adjudicated; everything in Sections 5 through 10 rests on them and on nothing else. **The chapter's central quantitative claims are therefore about the state of the literature, not about the size of an effect**, and are reported that way. The verdict table is filled but every cell is provisional pending retrieval.
**Last updated:** 2026-08-07

## 1. Verdict

The hypothesis is not currently supported by evidence that can distinguish it from its neighbours, and the reason is specific rather than general: almost no study is built to make the distinction. Of the schooling literature read at full text — the literature Caldwell's own formulation rests on — **not one paper separates the ideational mechanism from the economic one**. The single retrieved study designed to test the contextual-ideational claim on births reports a null.

| Phenomenon | Causal credibility | Demographic significance | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-modern | Not applicable | Not applicable | The mechanism requires an external society already identified with modernity. |
| First transition — historical West | Out of scope | Out of scope | The source of the diffused package, not a case of it. |
| First transition — diffused (post-1945) | **Very low** | **Not identified** | One study tests the contextual claim on births and finds nothing; one finds a contextual effect on marital timing, which is not a birth. |
| Second transition | **Very low** | **Not identified** | One cross-sectional study of stated ideals, at critical risk of bias. |

Three findings drive this, and the first two are findings about the field.

**The screen's primary cells did not survive full-text reading.** Nine retrieved records carried a primary or value-added cell after the blinded title/abstract screen. Six were overturned on reading — a 67% overturn rate. The pattern in the overturns is not random error but a single systematic mistake, described in §5.1.

**The value-added cell is empty.** Exactly one retrieved record sat in `DIFFUSION_INDEPENDENT_OF_STRUCTURE`, the cell holding designs that separate ideation from structure and therefore the only cell that can identify this hypothesis's distinctive claim. On reading it is a calibrated structural model of global fertility with no empirical ideational estimate. Nothing retrieved occupies that cell.

**Of eight schooling papers read in full, none decomposes its mechanism.** This is the number the chapter was built to measure, and §5.3 gives it in detail. It converts Caldwell's thesis from a contested claim into a largely untested one.

## 2. The Claim and Mechanism

The claim is that a belief can cause the thing it describes. A package of ideas about what it means to be modern — nuclear and conjugal rather than extended and kin-embedded, marrying late by choice rather than early by arrangement, spouses as equals, children few and heavily invested in — has spread from Western societies through mass schooling, mass media, development agencies, and direct contact. Where the package is absorbed, family aspirations move toward it, and fertility follows.

What gives the hypothesis content is the counterfactual attached to it. The claim is not that culture matters, which no one disputes, but that **the belief travels ahead of the structural change it forecasts and can act without it**. People come to believe that small families are what modern people have before the income growth, urbanization, mortality decline, or child-cost inflation that would make small families advantageous has arrived.

Caldwell routed this through mass schooling, arguing that a school restructures authority and obligation inside a household — relocating a child's day, deference, and future orientation away from the family economy — before it changes any wage or price. Thornton's developmental idealism is the general form: ordinary people hold a causal *theory* about development, propagated by states and international organizations, and acting on that theory is itself a cause of demographic change.

The identification problem follows directly. Western contact, schooling, media access, urbanization, income, and mortality decline arrive together, and every structural theory of fertility decline predicts the same correlation between exposure to modernity and low fertility. A raw association between modern ideals and small families is consistent with the entire literature. Only a design that holds structure fixed while ideation varies can separate them.

## 3. How the Evidence Is Organized

**The outcome level.** The package is a claim about the whole family form, so its earliest observable effects appear in marriage and residence rather than births.

| Outcome level | What it measures | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Realized fertility | Births, parity, completed family size | The decisive stratum |
| Stated intention or ideal | Intentions, desired or ideal family size | In scope, always flagged as stated |
| Family-formation behaviour | Age at marriage, spouse choice, nuclear residence | In scope as mechanism evidence |

**The three levels are never pooled**, and the pipeline emits them as three separate files so no later step can combine them. This turns out to matter more than anticipated: the three surviving studies sit at three *different* levels, so there is exactly one study per level and no pooling is arithmetically possible even before the rule is applied.

**Whether structural change is held fixed.** Every included paper records which of income, urbanization, child mortality, women's employment, schooling-as-human-capital, and contraceptive access its design adjusts for. The hypothesis's distinctive claim is independence from structural change, so an estimate's identification value is set entirely by this field.

## 4. Inclusion Approach

Twenty-eight existence- and version-verified anchor studies spanning four transmission channels — measured developmental-idealism beliefs, schooling as an ideational carrier, media carrying a modern-family model, direct Western contact — plus the theory canon and eight routing decoys, one per boundary. The anchors seeded a citation-based candidate pool of 4,701 deduplicated records.

**Six boundary walls, frozen before screening.** A paper leaves this chapter if its operative variable is an intergenerational *transfer* (C.3.f); a *self*-oriented value shift inside an already-rich society (D.1.a); the *geometry* of a diffusion channel rather than its content (A.20); *contraceptive* knowledge or legitimation (A.3); a woman's *own* autonomy (D.2.a); the *cost or return* to schooling (C.3.b, C.2.e); or a formally modelled *prestige-biased* copying process (D.1.c). The rule throughout: judge the estimand, not the framing sentence.

**Two scope decisions, both narrowing.** The first-transition cell covers only the *diffused* transition, because for the historical Western transition there was no external West to import a family model from; the West enters as the source of the package. And reduced-form schooling estimates that decompose no mechanism are recorded as `MECHANISM_UNRESOLVED_SCHOOLING`, reported as a count, and never pooled.

**A limit recorded in advance rather than discovered in an audit.** The scope stated before screening that a title/abstract screen cannot enforce the schooling wall, because mechanism decomposition lives in results tables. §5.3 is the full-text measurement that limit made necessary.

## 5. Included Studies and Evidence Map

Seventeen records were retrieved and read in full: nine carrying a primary or value-added cell from the screen, and eight from the unresolved-schooling stratum, drawn deterministically.

### 5.1 The screen's primary cells did not survive reading

| Study | Screen cell | After reading | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| de Silva & Tenreyro 2020, *AEJ:Macro* | `DIFFUSION_INDEPENDENT_OF_STRUCTURE` | Theory | A calibrated structural model, not an empirical ideational estimate |
| de Silva & Tenreyro 2017, *JEP* | Western contact | → A.5 | The operative variable is state population-control policy |
| Grimm et al. 2015, *Demography* | Media | → A.20 | Treatment is electrification; content unspecified |
| Tasciotti et al. 2024, *JODE* | Media | → A.20 | Same |
| Jaeger et al. 2018, NBER | Media | → out | US teen births; a source setting, and the content is teen-pregnancy programming |
| Liu & Kulu 2023, *IMR* | Western contact | → A.19 | Origin-norm persistence, not convergence with exposure |
| Kravdal 2000, *Demographic Research* | Schooling-ideational | **confirmed** | |
| Frye & Lopus 2018, *Demography* | Schooling-ideational | **confirmed** | |
| Pesando et al. 2025, *Social Forces* | Media | **confirmed** | |

Six of nine overturned. The overturns are not scattered: **five of the six are one mistake, made repeatedly.** The screen treated an exposure that *could* carry Western family content — a television, a migration, a development programme — as though it *did*. Electrification delivers television access; it does not establish what was watched. Migration places a person in Germany; it does not establish that convergence rather than persistence is being estimated. This is precisely the failure the walls were written to prevent, and an abstract is not enough to apply them, because an abstract states the treatment and rarely states the content.

The remaining overturn is different in kind and worse in consequence. The one record in the value-added cell — the cell carrying the chapter's distinctive claim — is a structural model. **No retrieved study occupies that cell.**

### 5.2 What survives

| Study | Setting | Design | Outcome level | Finding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kravdal 2000 | Zimbabwe, 1990–94 | Hazard models, ZDHS + census | Realized fertility | **Null.** District mean schooling and district literacy have no effect on a woman's birth rate beyond her own education, once urbanization is controlled |
| Frye & Lopus 2018 | 30 African countries | Multilevel, DHS | Family formation | Contextual effect present: the meaning of a woman's schooling depends on her agemates' attainment, shifting marital timing |
| Pesando et al. 2025 | India, unmarried women | Cross-sectional survey | Stated ideal | Online-technology exposure associated with shifted partnership ideals |

Three studies, three outcome levels, one each. The chapter's evidence base is one study per stratum.

Kravdal is the most informative of the three and deserves its weight stated plainly. It is built to ask the D.1.b question directly — does *community* education move fertility net of *individual* education? — which is the operational form of Caldwell's claim that schooling works through a shared reframing rather than through the schooled woman's own human capital. It finds nothing. And the direction of its residual confounding runs in the hypothesis's favour: district education is correlated with district development, so an unadjusted estimate would be biased *toward* the contextual effect the paper fails to find. A null that survives a bias pointing the other way is worth more than its risk-of-bias rating alone suggests.

### 5.3 The schooling literature does not decompose its mechanism

Eight papers from the unresolved-schooling stratum were read at full text, which is the only place a mechanism decomposition can be seen. **None decomposes ideational from economic mechanism. Zero of eight.**

The near-misses are instructive, because they show the gap is structural rather than accidental:

- **Osili & Long 2007** enumerates three candidate channels in its introduction — opportunity cost, contraceptive knowledge, female autonomy — and tests none of them separately. Enumerating channels is not decomposing them.
- **Choi 2018** genuinely decomposes, separating a "quantity effect" from a "return effect" — but both are economic. A decomposition between two economic channels does not answer whether the effect is ideational at all.
- **Baizan & Nie 2024** frames explicitly on Caldwell's wealth flows alongside microeconomics and gender equity, then tests differential effects *by parity*. Framing on Caldwell is not evidence for Caldwell.
- **Cummins 2025** cites Caldwell 1980's mass-schooling mechanisms by name, and still does not separate them from opportunity cost. It reports no causal effect of education on lifetime fertility.
- **Mocan & Cannonier 2012** comes closest to an ideational reading, showing schooling changed women's attitudes — but the attitudes concern health practices and violence against women, not family-model ideals, and no fertility estimate is split by channel.

The screen estimated 92% of the schooling evidence as mechanism-unresolved and flagged that as an upper bound, on the reasoning that abstracts rarely show decompositions. Full-text reading did not lower it. On this small sample it is 100%.

## 6. Quantitative Synthesis

**No pooled estimate is reported, and the reason is not that pooling failed.** The review's rule requires a common outcome family, a harmonised effect metric, and at least three independent studies. There is one study at each of the three outcome levels, and the levels may not be combined. The rule is not close to met at any level.

The pre-specified test of this chapter — whether an ideational effect survives adjustment for structural change — could not be estimated, because the cell holding designs capable of that test is empty among retrieved records. That test remains the right one and remains unanswered.

What can be said is narrow. Where the contextual-ideational claim has been tested directly on births, once, it did not hold. Where a contextual effect does appear, the outcome is marital timing rather than a birth. Where a media-content effect appears, the outcome is a stated ideal in a cross-sectional design at critical risk of bias.

## 7. Demographic Significance

No decomposition share, slope sufficiency, or R² is computed for either live phenomenon, and none should be. Demographic significance requires a causal effect size to scale against observed variation; there is one null, one non-fertility outcome, and one attitudinal association. Computing a share from these would give a number with no content.

This is a "not identified" verdict, not a "not significant" one. The distinction matters: the evidence does not show the mechanism is demographically trivial. It shows the mechanism has barely been tested in a way that could reveal its size.

## 8. External Validity and Transportability

The three surviving studies span sub-Saharan Africa (Zimbabwe; 30 DHS countries) and South Asia (India), which is the right part of the world for a hypothesis about diffusion into transitional societies — a better match than most chapters in this review achieve.

The structural feature that shapes everything is a trade-off between exposure measurement and identification. Thornton-style developmental-idealism instruments ask people directly what they believe about development and family, and are the best-measured exposure in the field; they are fielded in cross-sectional surveys. The best-identified designs — media rollouts, electrification, mission border discontinuities — proxy exposure with signal reach or distance and cannot say what belief changed. **Nothing retrieved sits in both categories.** Whether that trade-off is as sharp across the unretrieved literature is the single most valuable thing full retrieval would establish.

## 9. Risk of Bias and Identification Quality

Three studies assessed. Two serious, one critical.

The pattern matters more than the counts. All three share one problem: the exposure is not randomly assigned and is correlated with development. In Kravdal and Frye & Lopus the exposure is aggregate schooling, which rises alongside everything else that lowers fertility and delays marriage; in Pesando et al. the exposure is self-selected technology use, where women who seek online content plausibly already hold different ideals, and where exposure and outcome are measured in the same instrument.

Kravdal is the case where bias direction rescues the inference rather than undermining it, as described in §5.2. For Frye & Lopus the same confounding is not benign, because the result is positive and the confounder pushes the same way.

## 11. Interpretation for Economics Readers

The hypothesis proposes a preference change with an external cause and no accompanying price change: a belief about modern life enters the utility function directly, arriving from outside the economy, and moves desired family size before any budget constraint has moved.

The empirical content therefore rests on a narrow class of designs — those holding structural change fixed while ideational exposure varies. This review's main finding is that the class is close to empty, and that the literature which looks like it should populate it does not. The schooling literature is well identified: school-construction programmes, universal-primary-education reforms, college expansions, all yielding credible instruments. What it identifies is the total effect of schooling on fertility. Caldwell's claim is about *which channel that total runs through*, and an instrument for schooling cannot answer it. Better identification of the treatment does not help, because the treatment is not the disputed object.

There is an interpretive trap worth naming. A paper that opens by citing Caldwell, frames its motivation in ideational terms, and then estimates a reduced-form schooling coefficient is easily read as evidence for the ideational mechanism. It is not. Four of the eight schooling papers read here do exactly this, and one of them reports no causal effect on lifetime fertility at all.

## 12. Limitations and Extensions

**The binding limitation is retrieval, and it is severe.** Seventeen of 88 priority records were obtained (19%). Coverage is no better in the strata that matter: 1 of 6 in the value-added cell, 6 of 24 in realized fertility. The cause is specific — this literature is 1970s–2010s demography (*PDR*, *Demography*, *Population Studies*, *Rural Sociology*, *ASR*), a corpus predating open access and sitting behind JSTOR. Of 71 misses, 43 are genuinely closed and 28 are free to read but block automated clients. **Every count in Sections 5 through 10 should be read as conditional on retrievability**, and retrievability here correlates with recency and with economics-journal publication, which is exactly the kind of selection that could distort a comparison between an old demographic literature and a newer economic one.

**The screen is 54% complete**, so the pools will grow. Nothing suggests the composition will change — cell shares have been stable across three assemblies — but the counts are not final.

**One study per outcome level is not a synthesis.** The verdicts in §1 are the honest reading of what has been retrieved, not a settled assessment of the hypothesis.

Three things would move this chapter, in order of value. **Library retrieval of the 43 closed records**, a bounded task for a human with the UChicago proxy that would roughly triple the evidence base. **Any design holding structural change fixed while ideational exposure varies** — the empty cell is the chapter's real gap, and one good study would change the verdict more than fifty more schooling estimates. And **a schooling study that decomposes its own mechanism**, separating the ideational channel from opportunity cost, which is the study the entire Caldwell literature has been assumed to contain and, on this evidence, does not.

## 13. Reproducibility Appendix

**Coverage, stated once and plainly.** Citation frame 4,701 records. Screen 67 of 125 batches (1,675 of 3,123 abstract-bearing records); 1,578 title-only records assigned `INSUFFICIENT_INFO` by rule without a model call, and counted in every denominator. Retrieval 17 of 88 priority records. Full-text adjudication 17. **Studies supporting the verdict: 3.**

**Method faults found and fixed during this build**, recorded because they bear on how much the numbers can be trusted:

The existence-verification gate passed 8 of 28 anchors at the *wrong version* of the right paper — working papers, a preprint, a 2024 reprint, and a book review standing in for Thornton's 2005 monograph — all at title similarity 1.0. The gate catches titles resolving to nothing; it cannot catch titles resolving to the wrong real thing. The resolver now ranks candidates for version-of-record status, and the same fault was then found at the next stage and fixed there too. See `decisions/2026-08-07-version-of-record-gate.md`.

The screen validator, inherited from a chapter whose taxonomy had no sibling routers, initially rejected 22 of 40 correct verdicts by insisting a not-relevant paper carry no routing cell. A fail-closed validator that is itself wrong does not merely miss errors; it manufactures them and discards good work.

OpenAlex requests were unauthenticated across the whole goldset toolchain, drawing on a shared anonymous budget; a funded key sat unused in a `.env` that was not gitignored. Both fixed here, in this chapter's scripts only.
