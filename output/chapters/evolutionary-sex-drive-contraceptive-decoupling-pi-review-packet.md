## 1. What the chapter concludes

The hypothesis binds together two claims that the evidence pulls apart.

The **dissociation claim** is that the evolved link between social status and number of children
decays once contraception is available. This claim is well populated and now pooled.

The **distinctive decoupling claim** is that fertility falls because contraception severs sex from
reproduction, *with the desire for children held fixed*. This is what separates B.1 from the
modern-contraception chapter (A.2) and the preference-change chapter (D.1). No retrieved study
identifies it.

| Pre-modern, decoupling | Very low | Insufficient for variation |

## 2. The pooled estimate

Random-effects (DerSimonian-Laird) pooling on the Fisher-z scale, five studies, second-reader
verified against source tables.

| Cell | k studies | Pooled r | 95% CI | I-squared |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Male | 5 | +0.085 | 0.030 to 0.140 | 96.7% |
| Female | 3 | −0.128 | −0.212 to −0.041 | 96.5% |
| Overall | 5 | +0.006 | −0.058 to 0.070 | 99.8% |
| Contraception absent | 1 | not pooled (below the 3-study floor) | | |
| Contraception present | 4 | −0.014 | −0.080 to 0.054 | 99.8% |

The headline is the sex reversal, not a single number. Status predicts more children for men and
fewer for women, and the two cancel in aggregate.

**The demographic-significance reading, which is new in this draft.** A correlation of 0.085 tracks
0.73% of the variance in completed fertility; −0.128 tracks 1.63%. Because the sexes oppose, the
aggregate is indistinguishable from zero. A gradient that vanishes in aggregate cannot by itself
move aggregate fertility. I have written this into Section 7.3 as a finding that cuts *against*
reading the sex reversal as a large demographic force, rather than burying it.

## 3. Robustness checks I ran for this review

### 3.1 Does the 313,000-observation administrative sample drive the result?

This was the open weighting worry from the extraction session. It is answered, and the answer is no,
because the random-effects specification already neutralizes it.

| Study | n | Weight under fixed effects | Weight under random effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hopcroft 2018 (SIPP) | 313,405 | 94.6% | 23.2% |
| Kanazawa 2003 (GSS) | 12,084 | 3.6% | 22.7% |
| Hopcroft 2015 (NLSY79) | 2,880 | 0.9% | 21.1% |
| Fieder 2005 (Vienna) | 2,693 | 0.8% | 20.9% |
| von Rueden and Jaeggi 2016 | (meta, CI-based) | 0.1% | 12.1% |

With tau-squared at 0.0034, between-study heterogeneity swamps within-study precision and the
weights are nearly equal. A fixed-effect pool would have been almost entirely Hopcroft 2018 and
would have been the wrong specification here. This is worth stating because the instinct on seeing
n = 313,405 is that it must dominate, and under the chosen specification it does not.

### 3.2 Leave-one-out

**Male cell** (all five, r = +0.085):

| Omitted | k | Pooled r | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hopcroft 2015 | 4 | +0.088 | 0.021 to 0.155 |
| Kanazawa 2003 | 4 | +0.113 | 0.063 to 0.162 |
| Fieder 2005 | 4 | +0.065 | 0.004 to 0.125 |
| von Rueden 2016 | 4 | +0.071 | 0.013 to 0.129 |
| Hopcroft 2018 | 4 | +0.097 | **−0.004 to 0.196** |

**Female cell** (all three, r = −0.128):

| Omitted | k | Pooled r | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fieder 2005 | 2 | −0.102 | −0.192 to −0.010 |
| Hopcroft 2018 | 2 | −0.163 | −0.192 to −0.134 |
| Hopcroft 2015 | 2 | −0.117 | **−0.236 to 0.005** |

The point estimates are stable: male stays between +0.065 and +0.113, female between −0.102 and
−0.163, and no omission flips a sign. But **statistical significance in each cell rests on a single
study** (Hopcroft 2018 for men, Hopcroft 2015 for women); dropping it widens the interval across
zero. With k of 5 and 3 that is expected, and it is a reason to describe the result as a consistent
direction rather than a precise magnitude, which is how the chapter now words it.

### 3.3 The moderator the hypothesis actually turns on

The prediction is that the male status-to-reproduction link is positive where contraception is
absent and attenuates where it is present.

- Contraception **absent**: r = +0.19 (von Rueden and Jaeggi, one study)
- Contraception **present**, male: r = +0.071, CI 0.013 to 0.129 (four studies)

The direction matches the prediction and the gap is not small. But this compares one estimate
against a pool of four, not two pooled cells, so it is a suggestive contrast rather than a test, and
the conservative floor of three studies per cell means the absent cell is reported and not pooled.
The chapter currently withholds this contrast from the reader. See DECISION 3.

Note also that the present-cell male association remains **positive and significant**. The strong
form of the decoupling prediction, that the link should attenuate to zero under contraception, is not
what the data show; it attenuates but survives for men and reverses for women. The chapter says this.

## 7. DECISIONS REQUESTED

**DECISION 1. Pooling specification.** Confirm random effects (DerSimonian-Laird) as the
specification, given Section 3.1: it is what keeps a 313,000-observation administrative sample from
becoming 95% of the estimate. The alternative would be design-quality weighting, which would upweight
von Rueden and Jaeggi as the only moderate-risk study. *My recommendation: keep random effects, which
is also what the OAS chapter uses; do not add quality weighting, which would be a novel and
hard-to-defend weighting scheme applied to five studies.*

**DECISION 2. The `needs_pi` coefficients.** Zhang and Santtila 2022 (China CGSS, contraception
present) report standardized-predictor Poisson log-IRRs: income men B = +0.03, women B = −0.04,
sex-by-income interaction B = −0.06, all p < 0.001. These are the *only* extracted estimates that
directly test the sex interaction, and they reproduce the reversal in a non-Western setting. They are
not currently poolable because converting a Poisson log-IRR to a correlation requires an assumption.
Options: (a) leave out and cite narratively as external corroboration, (b) convert under a stated
assumption and include with a sensitivity check. *My recommendation: (a). The pool is small enough
that one converted estimate under a debatable assumption would carry real weight, and the narrative
citation gets the corroboration without the conversion risk.*

**DECISION 3. Report the absent-versus-present contrast?** Section 3.3 gives +0.19 (k = 1) against
+0.071 (k = 4), directionally exactly as the hypothesis predicts. The chapter currently withholds it
because one study is not a cell. Options: (a) keep withholding, (b) report descriptively with the
k = 1 caveat stated in the same sentence. *My recommendation: (b). Withholding a directionally
confirming contrast because it is underpowered risks looking like we buried the one result favorable
to the hypothesis. Reporting it with the caveat is more honest in both directions.*

**DECISION 4. Fieder within-study aggregation.** Fieder 2005 reports Spearman correlations
separately for six five-year age classes (male +0.07 to +0.22, female −0.10 to −0.27). I entered the
simple mean across age classes with total n per sex. A within-study fixed-effect pool of the six
would be more principled. *My recommendation: low priority. The leave-one-out shows dropping Fieder
entirely moves the male pool only to +0.065, so the aggregation rule cannot be load-bearing.*

**DECISION 5. von Rueden's target-phenomenon assignment.** It is assigned to the pre-modern cell by
fertility *regime* (33 non-industrial, pre-transitional societies) rather than by calendar date (the
constituent studies are twentieth-century fieldwork). Flagged `needs_human_review = yes` in the
target-period table. *My recommendation: keep the regime-based assignment, which is what makes a
population pre-transitional in substance, and keep the flag and the caveat sentence now in Section
7.1 so a reader sees the judgement rather than inheriting it silently.*

**DECISION 6. Is the chapter final, or does it wait on the absent cell?** Filling the
contraception-absent cell (historical and natural-fertility status-fertility studies, roughly 20 of
the outstanding targets) is the single highest-value extension and would let the availability
moderator be tested rather than described. It would sharpen the SDT rating. It would **not** rescue
the distinctive claim, which needs a design holding child preference fixed, and none exists in the
frame. *My recommendation: treat the chapter as final for review now and the absent-cell retrieval as
a scheduled revision. The verdict does not turn on it.*
