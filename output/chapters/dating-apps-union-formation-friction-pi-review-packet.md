## What the chapter concludes

No study has estimated the effect of dating apps on fertility. Of 10,739 records assembled from this
literature's citation neighbourhood, two report both an app-mediated exposure and a fertility outcome,
and they are one German panel study and its own working paper. That study finds no association with
first births in the cohort most exposed to apps, a positive association in the oldest cohort, higher
intentions to have a first child among those who met online, and — tested by its own authors —
a reverse relationship about three times the size of the forward one, in which wanting a child
predicts subsequent online partner-seeking.

Verdicts: pre-modern and First Demographic Transition **not assessed** (out of registry scope); Second
Demographic Transition **not assessed, insufficient evidence**. GRADE: technology-to-partnership **very
low**, apps-to-births **insufficient evidence**.

## Seven decisions for the PI

### 1. Does this chapter ship at 22% retrieval, or wait for band P0?

Band P0 holds fifteen records including the only identified estimate of app exposure on marriage (an
event study of Tinder diffusion in *Economics Letters*), a working paper titled *Wide and Shallow:
digital technology and the post-2007 fertility decline*, and identified estimates of broadband on teen
fertility and of adolescent internet exposure on age at first marriage. **Eight automated retrieval
rungs across two passes returned zero of the fifteen.** They need a human with a library proxy.

*RA recommendation:* ship the **empty-cell finding** now and hold the **direction** findings. The
claim that two of 10,739 records estimate the registered relationship is a property of the frame and
cannot move with retrieval. The claim that the evidence points against the hypothesis rests on
thirteen studies and could be reversed by the second record named above.

### 2. Is *not assessed — insufficient evidence* an acceptable sixth verdict?

The template's demographic-significance vocabulary offers negligible, minor, substantial, dominant and
not assessed, and defines the last as out-of-registry-scope. A.24 needed a case the vocabulary lacks:
in scope, but with no estimated parameter to compute a share from. **This will recur on every
hypothesis whose primary cell comes up empty**, so the decision belongs in
`docs/chapter-template.md` rather than in this chapter.

### 3. PI Call 2 left one sub-question open.

Sharing the broadband and internet-access studies with C.2.h did not settle whether **both chapters may
report the same magnitude** in their demographic-significance sections. The working rule adopted is a
shared evidence base with a single claimant on magnitude, named at synthesis. Without a rule of that
kind, two chapters sum the same coefficient into one aggregate decline.

### 4. The three-rater GRADE panel has not been run.

`literature/search-logs/dating-apps-union-formation-friction-grade-panel.md` contains one rater's
ratings plus two adversarial re-ratings by the same rater. **That is not independence and is labelled
as not being independence.** Both adversarial passes left the ratings unchanged and produced two
things worth the PI's attention: the age reversal in the best-designed study is a replicated
interaction rather than heterogeneity, and GRADE's floor cannot express that indirectness here means
*no evidence answers this question* rather than *the evidence answers a neighbouring one*. Rater
assignment requested.

### 5. The chapter's central claim rests on a working paper.

The reverse-causality finding — the fact the chapter is built around — comes from an INVEST working
paper. The version of record in the *Journal of Family Studies* is in band P0 and unread. If its
results differ, §6.2 and §9 change.

### 6. The timing bound is not computed from repository data.

`data/raw/` is empty in this working tree, so §7.3's statement that roughly half the United States
fertility decline predates swipe-based applications uses published national vital-statistics figures
rather than a series held here. The chapter marks it provisional. Building the series is a small task
and it belongs to whoever owns the data pipeline rather than to this ticket.

### 7. Is the registry entry itself now mis-specified?

A.24 as written asserts a fertility effect. The evidence base can only reach partnership, and the one
study reaching births reaches them the other way. **The chapter as drafted grades a link the registry
does not name.** Options: leave the registry alone and let the chapter state the gap, which is what it
currently does; or amend §A.24 in HYPOTHESES-v5.md to name union formation as the outcome and cite
A.7 for the remaining link.
