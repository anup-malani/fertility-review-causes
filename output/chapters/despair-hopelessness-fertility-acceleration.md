# Chapter: Foreclosed Futures and Accelerated Childbearing

**Category:** Cultural (psychological / existential)
**Primary mechanism:** Where the future holds no return to postponing a birth, the opportunity cost of an early birth collapses, and childbearing moves earlier rather than later — a child becoming an available source of meaning and adult status rather than an investment requiring a viable future.

## 1. The claim

Where economic opportunity is foreclosed, young women in the affected communities do not postpone
childbearing, because postponement buys them little. The claim is about the **timing and marital
context of first birth** — teen, adolescent and nonmarital childbearing — and its predicted direction
is **upward**. It concerns the Second Demographic Transition.

This is the same antecedent as the companion chapter and the opposite prediction. That is not a
contradiction to be resolved by averaging, and this review does not average it.

## 2. Theoretical mechanism

Two accounts converge, one economic and one ethnographic.

The economic account is Kearney and Levine (2014, *Journal of Human Resources*,
doi:10.1353/jhr.2014.0001), who find that low-SES young women are more likely to have a nonmarital
birth where lower-tail income inequality is greater, and propose a model of adolescent decision-making
to interpret it. The mechanism is a judgement about the *return to delay*: where the trajectory that
delay would purchase is not available, delay has no payoff and the opportunity cost of an early birth
falls toward zero.

The ethnographic account is Edin and Kefalas (2005, *Promises I Can Keep*), who document that poor
women in American cities place motherhood before marriage deliberately, treating it as the one
achievable source of meaning, identity and adult standing when the alternative life course has been
foreclosed. Wilson (1996) supplies the structural antecedent for both.

**What separates this chapter from its companion is what the despair is about**, not when it points.
Both mechanisms are forward-looking: the companion chapter's agent doubts the *capacity to provide*
for a child; this chapter's agent doubts the *return to waiting*. A present-versus-future distinction
would misdescribe both — the hypothesis list's own wording for the deferral claim is that "the
subjective sense of having a viable future disappears."

Modelled as an investment good, a child is deferred when the future is bleak. Modelled as a meaning
good available now, the same bleakness brings the child forward. Which model applies is an empirical
question about a population, not a theoretical question to be settled in advance.

## 3. Search strategy

Shared with the companion chapter — one search, one screen, split at extraction on the outcome's
margin. Section 3 of the deferral chapter states the method in full. Three points bear on this chapter
specifically.

**The outcome block carries this chapter's margin explicitly.** Retrieval terms include `teen birth*`,
`teenage pregnanc*`, `adolescent childbearing`, `early childbearing`, `early motherhood`,
`nonmarital`, `out-of-wedlock`, `first birth`, `age at first birth` and `postponement`. An outcome
block written only for completed quantum would have dropped this chapter's evidence at retrieval, and
the split would then have been an artefact of the query rather than a property of the literature.

**The chapter split runs on outcome margin, which the screen can enforce.** This is a fortunate
asymmetry: the wall this hypothesis cannot enforce at title and abstract is the one against C.5.a,
while the wall that separates the two chapters is visible in almost every abstract.

**This chapter's literature is roughly ten times the companion's.** Measured on the open index against
the production query: opportunity and inequality treatments appear in 4,800 records, chronic-decline
treatments in 474. The asymmetry has been visible since reconnaissance and should be expected in the
two ratings.

## 7. Demographic significance

**BLOCKED.** Requires stage 10 and a data retrieval step.

Two features of this chapter's computation differ from the companion's and are fixed now to prevent an
error at synthesis:

- **The denominator is teen and nonmarital births, not total births.** A ceiling computed against

  *against* the fertility decline this review is explaining. A significance verdict that summed the
  two chapters' magnitudes would double-count in the wrong direction.

## 10. Open questions and recommended studies

**The sign question is the review's, not the field's.** Both mechanisms can hold at once, in one
community, of different women — one deferring because she cannot provide, another accelerating
because waiting buys nothing. If that is what is happening, the aggregate sign of "despair on
fertility" is not a well-posed quantity at all, and the review's contribution is to say so precisely
rather than to report a pooled estimate that describes neither.

**What would sharpen it.** A study estimating both margins in one population — completed quantum and
timing of first birth, under one identification strategy — would settle whether the two mechanisms are
distinct populations or one population's two responses. The literature currently estimates them
separately, in different traditions, with different data.

**A known gap this review created.** The pre-2000 literature is out of scope by decision, not by
evidence. If this chapter's verdict turns on the strength of the American nonmarital-childbearing
work, the restriction should be revisited before the verdict is issued rather than after.
