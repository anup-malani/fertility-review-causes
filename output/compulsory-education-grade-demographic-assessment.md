## Child economic value — FDT

**Panel GRADE assessment: Low (3/3 raters; upgraded from Very low).** Shanan supplies one credible
historical-US quasi-experiment in which child-labor and compulsory-schooling restrictions reduce
child employment, increase attendance, and predict lower completed fertility and annual birth
probability. Exposure at ages 20--30 is associated with 0.301 fewer completed births (SE 0.044), and
a one-year higher average exit age with 0.108 fewer births (SE 0.024). State/cohort fixed effects,
state-specific trends, placebo exposure, migration checks, modern staggered-treatment estimators,
mortality checks, and neighboring-county comparisons strengthen credibility. The rating remains Low
because this is one nonrandomized historical study, adoption may retain residual endogeneity, the
laws bundle work and schooling restrictions, and mediation through lost child economic value is
inferred rather than estimated or independently replicated.

**Demographic significance: Partial; below the formal threshold.** Shanan explicitly calculates
that expanded exposure explains 8% of the 1900--1930 decline in annual birth probability among
30-year-old native-born women: `0.48 * 0.85 / 5 = 0.08`. This is a concrete, nontrivial contribution
but falls below the protocol's decomposition-share threshold of 10%. It is a back-of-envelope,
age-specific period-fertility calculation without a bootstrap interval, not a formal TFR or
completed-fertility decomposition. No alternative slope-sufficiency or conditional-R-squared route
meets a protocol threshold.

## Compulsory schooling as a teenage-birth/postponement driver

**Panel GRADE assessment: Moderate (3/3 raters; not reopened).** Multiple RD, IV, and policy-
discontinuity studies across the United Kingdom, North America, Turkey, Hungary, Thailand, India,
and Denmark consistently find that schooling reforms delay early births. Direct term-time and
conception evidence strengthens the incapacitation interpretation, while delayed marriage and
human-capital channels explain some heterogeneity. The evidence is less direct for permanent
fertility change and is not interpreted as such.

**Demographic significance: Insufficient data for aggregate FDT/SDT; meaningful for teenage-birth
timing.** No pooled estimate is justified and the extracted studies do not provide a common age-
specific fertility-rate scale that can be combined with historical reform exposure. The stream
therefore establishes a causal timing effect but does not yet meet the protocol's aggregate
decomposition, slope-sufficiency, or conditional-R-squared threshold.

## Panel reconciliation

PROTOCOL.md stage 11 requires three independent agent raters and escalation when ratings differ by
more than one level. After reading the full Shanan study, all three independently upgraded the child-
value rating from Very low to Low and changed demographic significance from Insufficient data to
Partial/below threshold. The teenage-birth assessment was not reopened. Maximum disagreement remains
zero levels, so no PI escalation is triggered.
