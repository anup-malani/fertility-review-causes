# Search scope — nationalist and pronatalist ideology

**Hypothesis:** D.1.d (HYPOTHESES-v5.md §D.1.d)
**Hypothesis slug:** `nationalism-pronatalist-ideology`
**Target phenomena:** FDT, SDT (per registry; not PM). Organized nationalist-pronatalist ideology is a modern-state phenomenon: it emerges with the interwar nation-states and authoritarian regimes of the First Demographic Transition (Fascist Italy's "Battle for Births", Nazi and Vichy natalism, Soviet motherhood cults, Francoist Spain) and recurs in the Second Demographic Transition as "demographic nationalism" (Hungary, Russia, Turkey, Iran's mid-1980s reversal, Israel, and post-2010 European and East-Asian pronatalist turns). It has no pre-modern expression, so PM is out of scope; the chapter carries FDT and SDT evidence.

Per the PI/RA decision of 2026-09-26 the fresh cross-field candidate scout was **skipped** and D.1.d taken directly on that prior. **The "smallest" claim is therefore a scouted prior from a *partitioned* scout (it never compared the economic C-section candidates), not the full measurement:** stage 3 must run the frame probe and the registered-construct completeness test (the A.6/A.3 lesson — see §"Registered-construct completeness check") before the ranking is trusted. D.1.d is a case where the prior deserves particular scrutiny, because the topic is (a) entangled with the pronatalist-transfer literature (C.2.d) and the coercive abortion/contraception-ban literature (A.4/A.2), and (b) sits behind two heavy homonyms — "national fertility" as a synonym for country-level fertility rates, and "population policy" as a catch-all — either of which a badly-built frame would annex.

## Causal claim

States sometimes try to raise the birth rate not by paying for children but by changing what having
children *means*. Through propaganda, patriotic rhetoric, honorific awards for large families, and the
moral framing of childbearing as a duty to the nation or the race, a regime attempts to persuade
citizens that reproduction is a civic or national act rather than a private choice. Where citizens
internalize this framing, a couple that would otherwise have stopped has an additional child out of a
sense of national obligation, emulation of the honored ideal, or alignment with a valorized collective
identity. Summed across a population, this **normative/ideological channel** raises fertility above the
level the same population would reach absent the campaign.

The registry attaches two strong qualifiers to the claim, and this chapter's job is to test them, not
assume them. First, the effect is expected to be **modest** — ideology alone does not change the budget
constraint, so its quantum footprint is bounded. Second, it is expected to be **mostly transient** —
much of any measured response is plausibly *tempo* (couples move a birth they would have had anyway
earlier, or comply for the duration of a campaign and revert afterward) rather than *quantum* (more
completed births per woman). A credible D.1.d estimate must therefore distinguish a durable level shift
in completed fertility from a temporary blip in period rates.

This chapter's mechanism is **ideational and state-sponsored** — a deliberately propagated norm — and
must be held apart from three things it is almost always bundled with in the real world:

- **the money.** Pronatalist campaigns are typically packaged with child allowances, maternity leave,
  childcare, and baby bonuses. Those financial instruments are **C.2.d (Tax and Transfer
  Pronatalism)**. D.1.d owns the framing, not the transfer.
- **the coercion.** Several of the most-cited "pronatalist" episodes worked not by persuasion but by
  *removing the means of avoiding birth* — banning abortion and contraception (Romania's Decree 770 of
  1966 is the canonical case). A supply restriction on fertility control is **A.4/A.2**, not ideology.
- **the diffuse value drift.** The broad secular, individualist, post-materialist value shift that
  lowers fertility is **D.1.a**; D.1.d is its deliberate, state-driven, opposite-signed counterpart, and
  the two must not be conflated.

The expressions of the ideology span the two modern phenomena:

- **Interwar and mid-century authoritarian natalism (FDT)** — Fascist Italy's "Battle for Births", Nazi and Vichy family ideology, Soviet motherhood medals and the cult of the mother-heroine, Francoist and other authoritarian population campaigns, generally coinciding with falling fertility in the FDT;
- **Contemporary "demographic nationalism" (SDT)** — post-2010 pronatalist-nationalist programs in Hungary, Russia, Turkey, Poland, and parts of East Asia; Israel's ethnonational pronatalism; Iran's mid-1980s ideological reversal — where explicit national/ethnic-demographic rhetoric accompanies (and this chapter seeks to separate from) financial packages;
- **The strength and framing of the appeal** — cross-national and temporal variation in the intensity of nationalist-pronatalist rhetoric, and individual-level variation in national identification and receptiveness to patriotic-duty framing, mapped to fertility intentions and behaviour.

## This chapter's parameter (the §4 chain)

D.1.d owns exactly one parameter: **the effect of state-sponsored nationalist/pronatalist *ideology*
(the normative framing of childbearing as a patriotic or national duty, propagated through propaganda,
rhetoric, and honorific awards) on realized fertility, holding fixed the financial incentives and the
coercive fertility-control restrictions that usually accompany it.** Concretely, the estimate this
chapter seeks is the number of additional births (or the higher completed/period fertility, or the
higher birth intention) attributable to the ideological framing itself, net of transfers and net of
abortion/contraception restriction, and whether that number is a durable quantum shift or a transient
tempo response. Three evidence streams identify it:

1. **Propaganda / campaign episodes with transfers held fixed** — regime-driven ideological campaigns whose fertility effect can be read off variation in *exposure to the rhetoric* rather than in the price of children: differential exposure to pronatalist propaganda (by media reach, region, cohort), or campaigns that changed rhetoric without a coincident change in transfers or abortion law. This is the design that can, in principle, isolate the ideational channel.
2. **Cross-national / temporal variation in the intensity of nationalist-pronatalist ideology** linked to fertility, net of policy — comparative-historical and panel studies of pronatalist rhetoric intensity (Demeny-style population-policy comparisons; Gauthier's cross-national family-policy-and-ideology work) that attempt to partial out the accompanying financial package.
3. **Individual-level national identity / patriotic-duty framing → fertility intentions and behaviour** — survey and framing-experiment evidence that national identification, patriotism, or explicit "duty to the nation" framing predicts or raises fertility intentions, holding economic circumstances fixed.

The chapter must hold fixed, and route elsewhere:

- **financial pronatalist instruments** — child allowances, parental leave, childcare subsidies, baby bonuses, and tax credits are **C.2.d (Tax and Transfer Pronatalism)**; D.1.d owns the framing that often accompanies them, and cites C.2.d as the co-bundled instrument, but a study identifying off the *transfer* is C.2.d evidence, not D.1.d;
- **coercive restriction of abortion / contraception** — a fertility rise driven by banning or restricting the means of fertility control is **A.4/A.2** (its coercion mirror); D.1.d owns persuasion, not prohibition;
- **the broad secular / individualist / post-materialist value shift** — the diffuse cultural drift that lowers fertility is **D.1.a**; D.1.d owns the deliberate, state-sponsored, pronatalist counter-framing;
- **generic religiosity → fertility** — religious pronatalism as a diffuse value (denomination, religious attendance, "be fruitful and multiply" as personal faith) routes to **D.1.a (secularization arm)**; D.1.d owns it only where the *state* instrumentalizes religious-national identity for an explicit demographic-national end;
- **cultural diffusion mechanisms** — the channels through which any norm (including this one) spreads — media reach, social networks — are **A.20**; D.1.d owns the *content* (nationalist-pronatalist framing), citing A.20 as the vehicle;
- **the economic roots of wanting births for the nation** — old-age security, labour-supply, or military-manpower motives as the *state's reason* for the campaign are context (JEL §2), and where a study identifies off pensions it is **C.3.c**.

State propaganda framing childbearing as national duty → fertility is this chapter. Cash and leave →
fertility is C.2.d. Abortion/contraception bans → fertility is A.4/A.2. The diffuse secular value shift →
fertility is D.1.a. How the norm spreads → A.20.

The completeness test in the probe prices each of these as `(baseline OR term) − baseline`. A large gain
from a genuine D.1.d construct (e.g. `pronatalism`, `patriotic duty`) means the baseline was under-built
and the term joins the frame honestly; a large gain from a generic word conjoined to nothing —
`national`, `population policy`, `nationalism` free-standing — is the free-`OR` inflater the A.3 park was
built on, and is reported as such, never folded in. The two catch-all homonyms enter **only** conjoined:

(see `HOMONYM`).

## Boundary walls (the load-bearing routing rules)

D.1.d sits at the junction of the cultural-ideology, fiscal-policy, and reproductive-coercion
literatures, so routing is the central screening task, and it is unusually hard here because the three
almost always co-occur in the same historical episode. Discriminators are stated at the level of the
*identified parameter*, not the keyword.

**Wall 1 — D.1.d vs C.2.d (Tax and Transfer Pronatalism). LOAD-BEARING.**
This is the wall the registry itself flags ("hard to separate ideology from accompanying transfers").
Pronatalist regimes bundle propaganda with money; a study of "pronatalist policy → fertility" usually
identifies off the *whole package*. The line is *what varies in the identification*. D.1.d owns the
effect of the **framing/rhetoric** holding transfers fixed; C.2.d owns the effect of the **cash/leave/
childcare price change**. Discriminator: a study identifying off a child allowance, baby bonus, leave
entitlement, or tax credit routes to **C.2.d**; a study identifying off a propaganda campaign,
patriotic-duty framing, or rhetoric intensity with the financial package held constant stays here. A
study that cannot separate the two (the common case) is a bundled-treatment study — recorded as
`MIXED_IDEOLOGY_TRANSFER` and read as an upper bound on the ideational channel, with the caution stated.

**Wall 2 — D.1.d vs A.4/A.2 (Abortion / Contraception access). LOAD-BEARING.**
The single most-cited "pronatalist" natural experiment — Romania's Decree 770 (1966) — worked by
*banning abortion*, not by persuasion; its fertility spike is an A.4 coercion effect, not evidence for
ideology. The line is *persuasion vs prohibition*. Discriminator: a study identifying off the
restriction or removal of abortion/contraception access routes to **A.4/A.2**; a study identifying off
ideological framing that leaves the means of fertility control untouched stays here. Coercion is not
ideology; the propaganda that accompanies a ban is D.1.d only if its effect can be read off separately
from the ban itself. Episodes that combine both (interwar and communist natalism routinely did) are
`MIXED_IDEOLOGY_COERCION` and flagged.

**Wall 3 — D.1.d vs D.1.a (Postmaterialism, Individualism, and Secularization).**
D.1.a owns the diffuse, bottom-up secular/individualist value shift that lowers fertility; D.1.d owns
the deliberate, top-down, state-sponsored nationalist framing that raises it. They are opposite-signed
and have different agents (spontaneous cultural drift vs organized state propaganda). Discriminator: a
study identifying off secularization, rising individualism, or post-materialist value change routes to
**D.1.a**; a study identifying off a nationalist-pronatalist campaign or patriotic framing stays here.
Studies where resurgent nationalism *reverses* a secular decline are a bridge cell: the exposure of
interest (nationalist ideology) is D.1.d, with D.1.a as the baseline it pushes against.

**Wall 4 — D.1.d vs generic religiosity / D.1.a secularization arm.**
Religious pronatalism ("be fruitful and multiply", high-fertility religious subcultures) overlaps
nationalist pronatalism where regimes fuse religious and national identity (religious nationalism).
Discriminator: a study identifying off *personal or denominational religiosity* → fertility routes to
**D.1.a** (secularization arm); a study identifying off the *state's instrumentalization of religious-
national identity for an explicit demographic-national goal* stays here. Diffuse faith is not D.1.d;
state religious-nationalist demographic projects are.

**Wall 5 — D.1.d vs A.20 (Cultural Diffusion Mechanisms).**
A.20 owns the *channels* through which any norm spreads — mass media, social networks, propaganda
infrastructure — independent of content. D.1.d owns the *content* — the nationalist-pronatalist norm
itself. Discriminator: a study whose object is the media/network channel (e.g. radio reach, propaganda
apparatus) *regardless of the message* routes to **A.20**; a study whose object is the pronatalist-
nationalist message's effect on fertility stays here, and may cite A.20 as the vehicle that carried it.

**Wall 6 — D.1.d vs the state's *motives* (military manpower, ethnic-demographic competition, economic
power).** Why a regime wants more births — geopolitical/military strength, ethnic-demographic
competition ("demographic race", great-replacement anxiety), or a larger labour force — is the
*motivation* for the campaign, not the exposure. Discriminator: these motives are context (JEL §2); the
exposure that identifies D.1.d is the ideological campaign/framing itself and its effect on citizens'
fertility. A study whose outcome is the *state's adoption* of a pronatalist stance (rather than
citizens' fertility) is `REVERSE`/context, not D.1.d evidence.

The C.2.d transfer wall (Wall 1) and the A.4 coercion wall (Wall 2) are the two most likely to need a
full-text call, and together they define the chapter's central identification problem: almost every
famous pronatalist episode is a bundled treatment.

## Eligibility rules

- **Include** if the study identifies the effect of nationalist/pronatalist *ideology* (a propaganda or
  patriotic-duty campaign, cross-population variation in rhetoric intensity, or individual national
  identification/patriotic framing) on realized fertility, completed or period fertility, or fertility
  intentions, in humans, holding fixed or attempting to separate the accompanying financial transfers
  and any coercive restriction of fertility control.
- **Include, bounded** if the pronatalist ideology is inseparable from transfers (`MIXED_IDEOLOGY_
  TRANSFER`) or from coercion (`MIXED_IDEOLOGY_COERCION`): these are the historical norm and are the
  chapter's main evidence, read as upper bounds on the ideational channel with the confound stated.
- **Exclude** if the estimand is a distal cause routed by a wall (a pure transfer effect → C.2.d; a pure
  abortion/contraception-ban effect → A.4/A.2; secularization/individualism → D.1.a; the diffusion
  channel regardless of message → A.20) with no ideology→fertility link of its own; if the outcome is a
  non-fertility consequence (voting, migration attitudes) or the state's *adoption* of pronatalism
  (`REVERSE`); if it is a homonym ("national fertility rate", "population policy" as a catch-all,
  citizenship, medical "natal", industrial "nationalization"); or if it is non-human.
- **Historical-demography and comparative-political evidence is in scope for FDT** (interwar and
  mid-century authoritarian natalism); the demographic-nationalism and framing-experiment evidence is
  the SDT stream. There is no PM stream.

## Identification cautions

- **The transfer confound (Wall 1) — the central problem.** Ideology travels with money. The registry's
  own note ("hard to separate ideology from accompanying transfers") is the defining caution: most
  estimates are of the whole pronatalist package, and the ideational share is an upper bound unless the
  design holds transfers fixed. The chapter's headline number must state whether it is the ideology
  alone or the bundle.
- **The coercion confound (Wall 2).** The most dramatic "pronatalist" fertility responses in the
  literature (Romania 1966 above all) are abortion-ban effects, not persuasion effects. A design that
  reads ideology off an episode that also banned abortion has not identified D.1.d.
- **Reverse causality — unusually strong here.** Demographic decline is itself a powerful *cause* of
  nationalist-pronatalist politics: regimes adopt pronatalism *because* fertility is low. A
  cross-section of "pronatalist regimes have low fertility" therefore has the sign backwards. Credible
  designs need exogenous variation in exposure to the ideology, not in the regime's decision to adopt
  it.
- **Tempo vs quantum / transience.** The registry expects "modest and mostly transient" effects. Much
  of any measured response is plausibly couples retiming a birth (or complying for a campaign's
  duration and reverting), not adding a completed birth. Period-fertility spikes must be checked against
  completed-cohort fertility before being read as a quantum effect.
- **Regime selection and external validity.** Organized pronatalist campaigns arise in specific (often
  authoritarian, or demographically anxious) regimes; the estimated effect is conditional on that
  setting and does not generalize to a liberal-democratic mean. Demographic significance must be
  assessed against the populations actually exposed, and weighted accordingly.
- **Measurement of ideology intensity.** "Intensity of nationalist-pronatalist rhetoric" is a
  constructed, often coder-judged variable; stated national-identification and patriotic-framing survey
  items carry social-desirability bias. Revealed-exposure designs (differential propaganda reach) are
  higher-quality than stated-attitude cross-sections.
