# Search scope — son preference and gender-biased fertility norms

## Causal claim

Couples who care about the sex of their children, not only the number, make fertility decisions that a
sex-indifferent couple would not. Where a culture attaches value to sons specifically — through the
patrilineal transmission of the family name, inheritance rules that favour male heirs, or the
expectation that a son (not a daughter) supports parents in old age — a couple that has reached its
desired family size but has no son faces a choice a sex-indifferent couple never faces: stop, or have
another child in the hope of a boy. Many choose to continue. Summed across a population, this
**differential stopping** raises completed fertility above the level the same population would reach if
it valued sons and daughters equally.

The effect has a second regime. Once prenatal sex-diagnosis and abortion make it possible to choose the
sex of a birth before it happens, the same preference can be satisfied without an extra birth: a couple
aborts female foetuses until it conceives a son. The preference then shows up as a **skewed sex ratio at
birth** rather than as extra children, and its upward pressure on fertility is partly or wholly
**substituted away**. This is why the sign of son preference's effect on the number of births is regime-
dependent: strongly positive under natural fertility and differential stopping, attenuated (and possibly
near-zero) where sex selection is cheap and available.

This chapter's mechanism is **cultural** (a gender-biased preference over the composition of offspring),
distinct from the demographic accounting of adult sex ratios (A.10) and from the technology of abortion
(A.4). It holds fixed the desired *number* of children a couple would have absent any sex preference and
estimates the fertility consequence of the sex preference itself.

The drivers and expressions of son preference span all three phenomena:

- **Differential stopping under natural or controlled fertility** — couples continue childbearing conditional on the sex composition of the children they already have; the classic signature is a higher probability of a further birth among parents of daughters only;
- **Substitution through sex-selective abortion** — where prenatal sex diagnosis (ultrasound, amniocentesis) is available, the preference is expressed as a skewed sex ratio at birth and the fertility effect attenuates;
- **The strength and geography of the norm** — son preference is quantitatively large in South and East Asia (China, India, Korea, Vietnam, the South Caucasus) and modest or absent elsewhere, so cross-population variation in the intensity of the norm maps to cross-population variation in its fertility effect.

## This chapter's parameter (the §4 chain)

D.2.c owns exactly one parameter: **the effect of son preference on realized fertility, operating
through sex-composition-conditional continuation (differential stopping), holding fixed the sex-
indifferent desired family size — and its attenuation by sex-selective abortion where available.**
Concretely, the estimate this chapter seeks is the number of additional births (or the higher
continuation/parity-progression probability, or the higher completed fertility) attributable to the
preference for sons, and how that number changes when sex-selection technology enters. Three evidence
streams identify it:

1. **Differential stopping / parity progression by sibling sex composition** — the workhorse micro design: the probability of a further birth, or completed fertility, as a function of the sex composition of existing children (Ben-Porath & Welch 1976; the sons-vs-daughters continuation-ratio literature; Yamaguchi; Arnold, Choe & Roy 1998). This identifies the behavioural signature within a population.
2. **Substitution: arrival of sex-selective abortion → fertility and sex ratio at birth** — natural experiments from the diffusion of ultrasound/amniocentesis: when sex selection becomes available and cheap, fertility falls and the sex ratio at birth rises (Chung & Das Gupta 2007; Ebenstein 2010; Lin, Liu & Qian 2014 on Taiwan amniocentesis; Jayachandran 2017). This identifies the regime-dependence of the fertility effect.
3. **Cross-population / macro variation in norm intensity** — survey measures of the desired sex composition of children (DHS ideal-sex-ratio items, "desired sex ratio at birth") linked to completed fertility or TFR across regions and cohorts.

The chapter must hold fixed, and route elsewhere:

- **the adult sex ratio as an exposure to the marriage market** — skewed numbers of marriageable men and women, and their effect on marriage timing and bargaining, are **A.10 (sex ratio imbalance and marriage-market effects)**; D.2.c owns son preference as a *cause* of fertility and a *cause* of the skewed sex ratio at birth, not the downstream marriage-market consequences of the resulting adult imbalance;
- **abortion access as a general remover of unwanted births** — the fertility effect of legal/available abortion regardless of sex is **A.4**; D.2.c owns only sex-selective abortion as the *expression* of the preference;
- **deliberate parity-specific stopping regardless of sex** — the FDT quantum signature of stopping at a target number is **A.8**; D.2.c owns stopping conditioned on the *sex composition*, not the number;
- **old-age security as the economic root of wanting sons** — pensions and formal old-age support substituting for sons is **C.3.c**; D.2.c owns the cultural preference, citing C.3.c as one of its roots;
- **general gender-equity / female-autonomy norms** — egalitarian norms that lower fertility overall are **D.2.a**; D.2.c owns the specific gender-*biased* norm that raises it;
- **child mortality and replacement** — continuing until a *surviving* son mixes son preference with the replacement effect of **A.1**; the sex-preference component is D.2.c, the mortality component is A.1.

Son preference → differential stopping / skewed sex ratio at birth → fertility is this chapter. The
adult sex ratio → marriage and fertility is A.10. Abortion in general → fewer births is A.4. Stopping at
a number → lower fertility is A.8. Why people want sons (old-age support) → C.3.c.

## Boundary walls (the load-bearing routing rules)

D.2.c sits at the junction of the cultural-norm, demographic-accounting, and reproductive-technology
literatures, so routing is the central screening task. Discriminators are stated at the level of the
*identified parameter*, not the keyword.

**Wall 1 — D.2.c vs A.10 (Sex Ratio Imbalance and Marriage-Market Effects). LOAD-BEARING.**
This is the wall the scout flagged: "sex ratio" appears on both sides. The line is *which sex ratio and
which direction of causation*. D.2.c owns son preference as a **cause** — of extra births (stopping) and
of a skewed **sex ratio at birth** (the newborn cohort's composition). A.10 owns the skewed **adult sex
ratio** as an **exposure** to the marriage market, and its downstream effects on marriage timing,
bargaining, and fertility. Discriminator: a study identifying off son-preference intensity or off the
arrival of sex-selection technology, with fertility or sex-ratio-at-birth as the outcome, stays here; a
study identifying off an already-skewed adult sex ratio (from war, migration, or a prior generation's
sex selection) acting on marriage and fertility routes to **A.10**. Sex ratio at birth as the newborn
*expression* of son preference is D.2.c; adult sex ratio as a marriage-market *shock* is A.10.

**Wall 2 — D.2.c vs A.4/A.2 (Abortion / Contraception).**
Sex-selective abortion is the technology through which son preference substitutes an aborted female
foetus for an extra birth; A.4 owns the general fertility effect of abortion access (removing unwanted
pregnancies of any sex). Discriminator: a study on abortion or contraceptive *access → total births*
regardless of sex routes to **A.4/A.2**; a study on sex-*selective* abortion as the expression of son
preference — differential fertility by preference, or the sex ratio at birth — stays here. Abortion is
the instrument; the preference is the cause.

**Wall 3 — D.2.c vs A.8 (Parity-Specific Stopping and Spacing).**
A.8 owns deliberate stopping at a target *number* of children, the FDT quantum signature, regardless of
sex. D.2.c owns stopping conditioned on the *sex composition* of the children already born.
Discriminator: a study whose stopping rule is a total-parity target routes to **A.8**; a study whose
stopping rule depends on how many sons (or daughters) the couple already has stays here. The sex-
composition interaction is the D.2.c fingerprint.

**Wall 4 — D.2.c vs C.3.c (Old-Age Security and Pension Crowdout).**
One economic root of son preference is that sons, not daughters, provide old-age support under
patrilineal norms. Discriminator: a study identifying off pension or formal old-age-security variation
routes to **C.3.c**; a study identifying off son-preference intensity stays here. C.3.c is a root cause
of the preference and is cited as such, but pension-driven fertility change is not D.2.c evidence.

**Wall 5 — D.2.c vs D.2.a (Female Empowerment and Gender Equity).**
Both are "gender norms," and the frame terms brush against each other. D.2.a owns egalitarian norms and
female autonomy that lower fertility broadly; D.2.c owns the specific gender-*biased* preference for
sons that raises it (and skews sex ratios). Discriminator: a study identifying off female
education/autonomy or egalitarian-norm diffusion routes to **D.2.a**; a study identifying off son
preference specifically stays here. Studies where rising female autonomy *erodes son preference* are a
bridge cell: the outcome of interest (son preference and its fertility effect) is D.2.c, with D.2.a as
the upstream force.

**Wall 6 — D.2.c vs A.1 (Child Mortality Decline, Replacement/Insurance).**
Under high mortality, couples continue until they have a *surviving* son, mixing son preference with the
replacement/hoarding response to mortality. Discriminator: a study identifying off mortality decline
routes to **A.1**; a study identifying off the sex preference, holding survival fixed or controlling for
it, stays here. A design that cannot separate "insuring against son death" from "wanting a son" has not
cleanly identified this parameter.

The A.10 sex-ratio wall (Wall 1) and the A.8 stopping wall (Wall 3) are the two most likely to need a
full-text call.

## Eligibility rules

- **Include** if the study identifies the effect of son preference (or its intensity, or the arrival of
  sex-selection technology) on realized fertility, parity progression/differential stopping, completed
  fertility, or the sex ratio at birth *as the substitution expression of the fertility effect*, in
  humans, holding fixed or controlling for the sex-indifferent desired number of children.
- **Exclude** if the estimand is a distal cause routed by a wall (adult sex-ratio marriage-market
  effects, general abortion access, sex-indifferent parity stopping, old-age security, general gender
  equity, or child mortality) with no son-preference→fertility link of its own; if the outcome is a
  non-fertility consequence of son preference (child health, schooling, nutrition, excess female
  mortality — real and important, but not this chapter's parameter); if it is a homonym; or if it is
  non-human.
- **Natural-fertility and historical-demography evidence is in scope for PM/FDT** (historical parish and
  genealogical reconstitutions of sex-conditional stopping; pre-ultrasound high-fertility settings);
  the sex-selection-substitution and DHS-norm-intensity evidence is the FDT/SDT stream for South and
  East Asia.

## Identification cautions

- **Reverse causality and joint determination.** Realized family size and the observed sex composition
  are jointly produced; the cleanest differential-stopping designs use the randomness of the sex of
  early births (the sex of the first child is close to random absent selection) as the source of
  variation, and test parity progression conditional on it.
- **The substitution confound (Wall 1/Wall 2).** Where sex selection is available, the fertility effect
  and the sex-ratio-at-birth effect move in opposite directions; a study that measures only fertility
  will understate the preference's strength, and a study that measures only the sex ratio says nothing
  about the number of births. The two outcomes must be read together to recover the regime-dependent
  parameter.
- **The mortality confound (Wall 6).** Under high child mortality, continuation until a surviving son
  blends son preference with replacement; designs must hold survival fixed or control for it.
- **Measurement of norm intensity.** Stated desired-sex-composition items (DHS) are subject to
  social-desirability and rationalisation bias (parents may report wanting the children they have);
  revealed-preference designs (differential stopping, sex ratio at birth) are higher-quality than
  stated-preference cross-sections.
- **External validity.** The parameter is large in South and East Asia and near-zero in most of the
  West; a pooled global estimate is not meaningful. Demographic significance must be assessed per
  region, and the aggregate world contribution weighted by the population living under the norm.
