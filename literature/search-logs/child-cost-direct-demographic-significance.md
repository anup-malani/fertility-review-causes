**Observed US fertility change 1967–2024: TFR 2.56 → 1.63, -36.4%.**

## The elasticity

`-0.2` — Ebenstein, Hazan & Simhon (2015), Israeli kibbutz privatisation: ~0% -> ~100% parental cost share, lifetime fertility -0.59 children = ~20% of the 3-child mean. Arc elasticity ~ -0.2, used as an UPPER bound.

It is used because it is the only estimate the chapter has inside its registered SDT window. Every number below inherits its weaknesses, and the two computations differ by more than the elasticity is precise to.

## Verdict input — the two readings, and why they cannot both hold

Correcting the window turns this from one number into a contradiction, and the contradiction is the result.

**Reading 1 — over the whole window, the price barely moved.** On the long-run arm (1967–2023, the only one spanning the window from its start, because tuition series begin in 1978) the real price of children rose **+10.9%** while TFR fell **-36.8%**. At an elasticity of -0.2 that is **13%** of the decline. The mechanism is real and small.

**Reading 2 — after 1978 the price moved enormously and fertility barely responded.** US TFR fell only **-8.1%** between 1978 and 2023, while the education-bearing arms of the price index rose 79–205% in real terms. Applying the same elasticity implies a fall of 15–41%, i.e. **365%** of what actually happened on the most favourable arm. The mechanism does not under-predict here; it **over-predicts by two to four times**.

**Both readings cannot be true of the same parameter, and neither helps the hypothesis.** Either the index overstates the price parents actually face — likely, since what carries it is college tuition and a K-12 series priced off private schooling, neither of which is the required outlay most parents meet — or the kibbutz elasticity does not transport, which its own authors warn about when they say the elasticity 'may not be constant'. On the reading most generous to the index, the mechanism should have emptied the maternity wards; on the reading most generous to the elasticity, it accounts for roughly a tenth of the fall.

**Strip education out and the sign flips**: the `without_education` arm falls 16.6% in real terms, implying fertility should have RISEN. Scope ruling 2 assigns college tuition largely to C.3.d as chosen quality rather than to C.2.b as a required price, so the component doing the work is the one this chapter has the weakest claim to.

Every number rests on an elasticity from a single MODERATE-risk study of an extreme treatment in an unusual population, extrapolated to marginal price changes the design never observed. These bound the mechanism; they do not estimate it.
