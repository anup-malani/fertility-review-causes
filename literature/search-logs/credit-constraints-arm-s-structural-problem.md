**TICK-077 · 2026-09-01 · Shravan** · **FOR ANUP — this supersedes PI Call 1 and is larger than it.**

Four Arm S records were read at full text today, the first of the arm to be read at all. **None
survived as C.3.e.** The pattern in how they failed is the finding.

## What the four turned out to be

| Study | Screened as | What actually varies | Routes to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ridker, *The No-Birth Bonus Scheme* (1980) | `PRIMARY_SAVE_INSURE` | **the price of a birth** — the scheme pays into a savings account for each year without one, in the paper's words "compensation for the loss incurred in forgoing additional children" | **C.2.d** |

The savings account in the Ridker scheme is the **payment vehicle**, not a change in the household's
asset menu. Under the rule this project runs on — hypotheses are separated by *what varies* — that is
C.2.d's exposure, not Arm S's.

**Arm S: 16 records → 12.** And the four read are the four that looked most directly on-estimand.

## The larger problem: Wall 1 cuts on an axis the founding literature does not have

Wall 1 splits the asset motive by **which risk is insured** — longevity to C.3.c, within-life income
and health risk to C.3.e. Cain's reply shows that distinction is ours, not the literature's:

> "I take insurance to mean a form of protection (not necessarily perfect) against the adverse
> economic consequences of a potentially threatening event or contingency. Once this is understood,
> there is no conceptual difficulty in treating children as insurance against risk."

He names disability, widowhood and women's dependent status, depredation and floods **alongside old
age**, as instances of one concept, and cites **Nugent's "The old-age security motive for fertility"
approvingly in support**. The founding literature treats old-age security as *one case* of the
insurance motive, not a separable category.

So Wall 1 divides a concept its own sources treat as unitary. That is the wall-cut-on-the-wrong-axis
failure, and the earlier probe missed it because it measured **vocabulary** (how many asset-motive
records carry old-age words: 13.7%) rather than **what the studies vary**.

## Three ways out, and my recommendation

1. **Merge Arm S into C.3.c.** Clean, but it contradicts the registry, which gives C.3.e an explicit
   PM/FDT insurance configuration, and it leaves C.3.e as a one-arm liquidity chapter.
2. **Re-cut Wall 1 on the instrument, not the risk.** C.3.c owns variation in **non-child old-age
   security provision** (pensions, social pensions); C.3.e Arm S owns variation in **general financial
   instruments** (savings access, insurance access, credit access) whatever risk they cover. This
   keeps both hypotheses and uses the project's governing rule rather than a bespoke one.
3. **Accept that Arm S is mostly empty of admissible variation.** Under rule 2, most of the classic
   literature still falls out — it varies *risk exposure* (Cain), *land* (Stokes), or *the price of a
   birth* (Ridker), not instrument access. What remains is the modern financial-access work, which is
   the composite cell.

**I recommend 2, and expect it to deliver 3.** Re-cutting on the instrument is principled and
consistent with C.2.c's precedent. But applied honestly it will route most of the historical Arm S
records out, and Arm S will end up resting on the same financial-access studies as the composite
cell — which would mean the two-arm structure is real in theory and nearly empty in practice on the
S side.

**What this does not change.** Ruling 1's logic stands: composite exposures still cannot be allocated
between saving and borrowing channels, so the chapter is still one chapter. And Arm B is untouched —
its exposure (borrowing terms) is unambiguous and its records vary it.

### The cost, stated plainly

**The re-cut demotes the rainfall paper** — the study I had ranked as the single highest-value
outstanding retrieval in the chapter, and the only plausibly identified Arm S design in the pool. What
varies in it is rainfall risk across frontier settlements, not access to any financial instrument, so
under the rule we have just adopted it is **C.5.a's**. Consistency requires it; it is still worth
retrieving, for C.5.a.

That leaves **Arm S with five records**, of which one is retrieved and extracted (US counties c.1850),
one is unavailable (19th-century Britain), and three are unread. And the US-counties study survives
*because* of the re-cut: bank presence is a financial instrument becoming available, even though its
authors frame the result as old-age security. Framing is not what varies — which is exactly the point
of cutting on the instrument.

### What this means for the chapter

- **Arm S cannot support a GRADE rating as it stands**: five records, one read.
- **PM and FDT hang on it.** If Arm S stays at five with one retrieved, the honest verdict for those
  phenomena is **UNEVALUATED with GRADE No evidence**, not a rating.
- **Ruling 1 still stands.** Composite exposures remain unallocable between saving and borrowing, so
  this is still one chapter — but the S side of the two-arm structure is now thin enough that the
  chapter should say so in the verdict rather than presenting two balanced arms.
- **Arm B (13) and composite (32) are untouched.**
