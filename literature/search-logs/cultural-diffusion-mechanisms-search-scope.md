## Causal claim

Once a norm about family size begins to move — whatever the norm is and whatever made it move — *how
fast* it spreads and *where* it spreads depend on the architecture through which people are connected:
who talks to whom, what media reach them, and where the boundaries of a linguistic or cultural
community fall. A.20 is the claim that these **channels of transmission** are themselves a cause of the
pace and geography of fertility change — that the same underlying shift in desired family size diffuses
quickly and broadly where networks are dense and media reach is wide, and slowly or not at all where the
channels are absent or a community boundary blocks them.

The hypothesis is registered as **proximate and content-agnostic**. It does not ask *what* norm is
spreading or *why* couples came to want smaller families — that is D.1 (the value shift) and A.3 (the
diffusion of fertility-*control* knowledge specifically). A.20 owns only the vehicle: the effect of the
channel on the timing and spatial pattern of change, holding the message fixed. Its canonical claim is
that fertility transitions cluster and time themselves by the shape of the communication network, not
only by the distribution of their economic and cultural causes.

The registry attaches a strong qualifier to the claim, and this chapter's job is to test it, not assume
it. A.20 is explicitly a claim about **timing and geography, not direction or cause**: it explains why a
transition happened *here before there*, or *fast rather than slow*, but not why desired fertility fell
in the first place. A credible A.20 estimate therefore identifies the effect of a channel — media reach,
network position, community boundary — on fertility (or on the *speed* of its change), and must not be
read as evidence for the content the channel carried.

This chapter's mechanism is the **channel**, and it must be held apart from the two things it is almost
always confounded with:

- **the content.** The spread of the specific idea and legitimacy of *deliberate fertility control* is
  **A.3 (Diffusion and Social-Learning of Fertility Control)**. A.20 owns the conduit; A.3 owns what
  travels down it. This is the load-bearing wall (Wall 1), and it is the mirror of A.3's own Wall 1.
- **the cause.** *Why* desired family size changed — secular/individualist value drift (**D.1.a**),
  state nationalist framing (**D.1.d**), the economics of children (the C-section) — is the content and
  root cause. A.20 is agnostic to all of it; it explains the diffusion, not the demand.

The expressions of the mechanism span the two modern phenomena:

- **Linguistic and cultural boundaries (FDT)** — the Princeton European Fertility Project residual:
  marital-fertility decline clustered by language group and religion and crossed provincial and national
  borders on a schedule that income, urbanization, and mortality do not reproduce (Coale & Watkins 1986;
  Watkins 1991). Read as the community boundary acting as a *conduit and a barrier* to spread.
- **Mass media and social networks (SDT)** — the modern micro-causal core: entertainment-media rollouts
  that carried an ambient small-family portrayal (Brazil Globo *novela* coverage — La Ferrara, Chong &
  Duryea 2012; India cable television — Jensen & Oster 2009), and social-network adoption studies where
  a couple's contraceptive/fertility behaviour responds to network partners' behaviour (Kohler, Behrman
  & Watkins 2001).
- **Network architecture and channel reach as variation** — cross-population and temporal variation in
  the density of social ties, the penetration of radio/TV/cable, and the permeability of community
  boundaries, mapped to the pace and spatial pattern of fertility change.

## This chapter's parameter (the §4 chain)

A.20 owns exactly one parameter: **the effect of the diffusion channel — the architecture of social ties,
the reach of mass media, and the permeability of linguistic/cultural community boundaries — on realized
fertility and on the pace and geography of its change, holding fixed the *content* of the diffusing norm
and the *underlying reason* desired fertility changed.** Concretely, the estimate this chapter seeks is
the effect on fertility (or on the speed of its change) of exposure to a channel — mass-media reach (TV
or radio signal, cable rollout, *novela* coverage), position in or density of a social network, or
membership in a linguistically/culturally bounded community — where identification comes from variation
in *the channel itself*, not in the message it carried and not in the fundamentals that changed demand.
Three evidence streams identify it:

1. **Mass-media channel** — the cleanest micro-causal cell. Quasi-experimental variation in media reach
   whose programming carried an *ambient* (not deliberately instructional) small-family portrayal:
   Brazil's Globo *novela* signal coverage (La Ferrara, Chong & Duryea 2012), India's staggered cable-TV
   introduction (Jensen & Oster 2009), radio signal reach. Identification is off *coverage/signal*, and
   the message is entertainment, not a family-planning PSA — which is what keeps it A.20 and not A.5/A.3.
2. **Social-network / peer-effect channel** — network position and peer behaviour predicting or raising a
   couple's own fertility/contraceptive adoption, holding content fixed (Kohler, Behrman & Watkins 2001
   on Kenyan and Malawian networks; Montgomery & Casterline). The reflection problem (Manski) is the
   central threat and the discriminator between an identified estimate and a descriptive correlation.
3. **Linguistic / cultural-boundary channel** — the Princeton signature: decline clustering by language
   and religion and crossing borders on a timetable development does not reproduce (Coale & Watkins 1986;
   Watkins 1991), read as evidence for the *boundary as conduit and barrier*, net of the fundamentals.

The chapter must hold fixed, and route elsewhere:

- **the diffused content — fertility-control knowledge and its legitimacy** — the spread of the idea and
  acceptability of *deliberately limiting births specifically* is **A.3**; A.20 owns the channel it rode
  on, and cites A.3 as the content, but a study whose object is the spread of fertility-control knowledge
  itself is A.3 evidence, not A.20;
- **vertical (parent-to-child) transmission** — persistence of fertility preferences down generations is
  **A.19**; A.20 owns horizontal spread across peers, media, and communities;
- **the physical contraceptive technology** — the spread of methods (the Pill, the IUD) as a technology
  and its access is **A.2**; A.20 owns the spread of norms/information, which historically ran on
  withdrawal and abstinence and needed no new device;
- **organized family-planning programs** — exogenous program supply, including *deliberate* information-
  education-communication (IEC) mass-media campaigns with family-planning content, is **A.5**; A.20 owns
  the content-agnostic channel, and a program's diffusion *spillover* through social contact is the bridge
  cell (stays here only where the object is the channel);
- **the value shift / root cause** — *why* desired family size changed (secularization/individualism →
  **D.1.a**; state nationalist framing → **D.1.d**; the economics of children → the C-section) is the
  content; A.20 is agnostic to it and owns only the vehicle.

How a fertility-relevant norm spreads — the media, the network, the community boundary, regardless of the
message — is this chapter. The spread of fertility-*control* knowledge specifically is A.3. Parent-to-
child transmission is A.19. The spread of the *device* is A.2. A deliberate family-planning media campaign
is A.5. Why desired fertility fell is D.1 / the C-section.

The completeness test in the probe prices each of these as `(baseline OR term) − baseline`. A large gain
from a genuine A.20 construct (e.g. `mass media`, `peer effects`) means the baseline was under-built and
the term joins the frame honestly; a large gain from a generic word conjoined to nothing — `diffusion`
(physics, technology, marketing), `network` (social-network *analysis* of anything), `media`, `television`
free-standing — is the free-`OR` inflater the A.3 park was built on, and is reported as such, never folded
in. The homonym-heavy words enter **only** conjoined with a fertility outcome (see `HOMONYM`): `diffusion`
never stands free of a channel/network sense and a fertility outcome, and `social network` / `network`
never stand free of the substantive channel reading (as opposed to social-network *methodology*).

## Boundary walls (the load-bearing routing rules)

A.20 is a mechanism/channel hypothesis, so by construction its frame overlaps every *content* hypothesis
that diffuses — A.3 (fertility control), D.1.a (secular values), D.1.d (nationalism) — plus the sibling
*spread* hypotheses (A.19 vertical, A.2 technology, A.5 programs). Routing is therefore the central
screening task, and every wall turns on the same axis: **does the study's identification come from the
channel (A.20) or from the content/cause (the neighbour)?** Discriminators are stated at the level of the
identified parameter, not the keyword.

**Wall 1 — A.20 vs A.3 (Diffusion and Social-Learning of Fertility Control). LOAD-BEARING.**
This is the wall the registry flags and the mirror of A.3's own Wall 1 (A.3 resolved it on 2026-09-18 in
favour of channel-excluded A.3). A.3 owns the diffused *content* — the knowledge and legitimacy of
deliberately limiting births. A.20 owns the *channel* that content rides on — the media, the network, the
community boundary — regardless of the message. Discriminator: a study whose object is the media/network
channel *irrespective of the message it carried* — above all a mass-media *reach* study or an
entertainment-media rollout — routes **here**; a study whose object is the spread of fertility-control
knowledge/legitimacy specifically routes to **A.3**. The precedent is settled and runs in A.20's favour:
the Brazil *novela* and India cable-TV quasi-experiments, the cleanest identified estimates in the
neighbourhood, were routed **to A.20** at A.3's screen, because their treatment is signal coverage of
general entertainment, not a family-planning message. A study that cannot separate channel from content
(e.g. a media campaign that both reached people *and* carried an explicit small-family message) is a
bundled-treatment study — recorded as `MIXED_CHANNEL_CONTENT` and read as an upper bound on the channel
effect, with the caution stated.

**Wall 2 — A.20 vs A.19 (Intergenerational Transmission of Fertility Preferences).**
A.19 owns *vertical* transmission — parent-to-child persistence of family-size preferences. A.20 owns
*horizontal* spread — across peers, media audiences, and community members. Discriminator: a study
identifying off parent-child correlation or ancestral-culture persistence (the epidemiological/immigrant
design) routes to **A.19**; a study identifying off horizontal channels (media reach, peer/network
position, community boundary) stays **here**. A.19 is also the next-smallest scout candidate after A.20,
so this wall doubles as the check that the two frames are genuinely distinct.

**Wall 3 — A.20 vs A.2 (Contraceptive Technology and Diffusion).**
Both say "diffusion." A.2 is the spread of the physical *methods* (the Pill, the IUD) and access to them;
A.20 is the spread of *norms and information* through channels, which in historical Europe ran on
withdrawal and abstinence and needed no device. Discriminator: a technology-access or method-availability
design routes to **A.2**; a channel study (media/network/boundary) carrying norms rather than devices
stays **here**.

**Wall 4 — A.20 vs A.5 (Family-Planning Programs).**
A.5 is exogenous program *supply*, and it explicitly includes *deliberate* IEC / mass-media family-
planning campaigns — media with a specific, instructional family-planning message. A.20 is the content-
agnostic channel. Discriminator: a study identifying off a program's deliberate family-planning media
campaign (message-specific) routes to **A.5** (content) or **A.3** (if the object is the legitimacy that
spread); a study identifying off content-agnostic media *reach* (does having TV/radio/cable at all move
fertility, whatever the programming) stays **here**. Bridge cell: a program's diffusion *spillover* to
untreated neighbours through social contact is A.20's where the object is the channel of spillover, and is
recorded `PRIMARY_NETWORK_PEER` with the program noted.

**Wall 5 — A.20 vs D.1 (the value shift: D.1.a secularization, D.1.d nationalism — the content/cause).**
The mirror of D.1.d's Wall 5. D.1 owns *what* norm is spreading and *why* desired fertility changed
(secular/individualist drift → D.1.a; state nationalist framing → D.1.d). A.20 owns *how* any such norm
spreads. Discriminator: a study whose object is the message's effect on fertility (secularization →
fertility; nationalist framing → fertility) routes to **D.1**; a study whose object is the channel
(media/network/boundary) *regardless of the message* stays **here**, and may cite D.1 as the content it
carried.

**Wall 6 — identified diffusion vs descriptive clustering (the reflection problem — an identification
boundary, not a sibling).** Spatial, linguistic, or network *clustering* of fertility decline is
consistent with channel-driven diffusion, but equally with the clustering of the decline's own economic
and cultural causes (a common shock hitting connected people together — Manski's correlated effects) or
with homophily in network formation. A study that establishes clustering without exogenous variation in
the channel has *not* identified A.20's parameter, however tight the correlation. Discriminator: only
designs with exogenous channel variation (staggered media rollout, signal-geography discontinuities,
plausibly-exogenous network position) enter the identified core; pure clustering/correlation is the
descriptive residual (`MECHANISM_CHANNEL_DESCRIPTIVE`), reported but not pooled. This mirrors A.3's
identified-core-vs-descriptive-residual split (44 of 252, of which 2 of 10 retrieved identified by design).

Wall 1 (A.3, content vs channel) and Wall 4 (A.5, message-specific vs reach) are the two most likely to
need a full-text call, and together with Wall 6 they define the chapter's central problem: distinguishing
the effect of the *conduit* from the effect of the *message* and from the *clustering of fundamentals*.

## Eligibility rules

- **Include** if the study identifies the effect of a diffusion *channel* — content-agnostic media reach,
  social-network position/peer behaviour, or a linguistic/cultural community boundary — on realized
  fertility, contraceptive adoption, or the pace/geography of fertility change, in humans, where the
  identification is off the channel and not off the specific message or the fundamentals that changed
  demand.
- **Include, bounded** if the channel is inseparable from a specific message (`MIXED_CHANNEL_CONTENT`):
  read as an upper bound on the channel effect with the confound stated.
- **Exclude** if the estimand is a distal content/cause routed by a wall (the spread of fertility-control
  knowledge specifically → A.3; vertical transmission → A.19; contraceptive technology → A.2; a family-
  planning program message → A.5; a value-shift message → D.1.a/D.1.d) with no channel→fertility claim of
  its own; if the identification is pure clustering/correlation with no exogenous channel variation
  (`MECHANISM_CHANNEL_DESCRIPTIVE` — reported, not pooled); if the outcome is non-fertility (`OFF_OUTCOME`)
  or the direction is reversed (`REVERSE`); if it is a homonym (`diffusion` in the physical sciences,
  social-network *methodology*, unrelated media/communication research); or if it is non-human.
- **Historical-demography and comparative evidence is in scope for FDT** (the Princeton linguistic/
  religious-boundary signature); the mass-media and social-network evidence is the SDT stream. There is
  no PM stream.

## Identification cautions

- **The channel-vs-content confound (Wall 1) — the central problem.** A.20's whole claim is that the
  conduit matters *regardless of the message*. Most media/network studies confound the two: the channel
  that reached people also carried a specific message. The cleanest identification uses *entertainment*
  media whose small-family portrayal is ambient rather than instructional (the Brazil *novela* design) or
  content-agnostic signal reach; a deliberate family-planning campaign identifies content (A.3/A.5), not
  the channel. The chapter's headline estimate must state whether it is the channel alone or the bundle.
- **The reflection problem (Manski) — the defining threat to the network stream.** Connected people adopt
  together both because one imitates the other (true diffusion) and because a common shock hits them
  together (correlated effects), and network ties themselves form by homophily (contextual effects). A
  network correlation identifies A.20 only if it separates the endogenous social-interaction effect from
  these. This is A.20's counterpart to the reflection problem that A.3 flagged as its central
  vulnerability.
- **Descriptive clustering is not identified diffusion.** Spatial/linguistic clustering of fertility
  decline (the Princeton residual) is consistent with channel-driven diffusion but also with the
  clustering of the decline's own causes. The FDT boundary evidence is powerful description; it becomes
  identification only with a design that isolates the boundary as a conduit net of fundamentals.
- **Reverse causality.** Media penetration and network formation correlate with modernization and with
  the very changes in desired fertility that A.20 is meant to be agnostic about; declining fertility can
  also change household media adoption. Credible designs need exogenous variation in the channel.
- **External validity.** The strongest estimates are specific rollouts (Brazil Globo coverage, India
  cable staggering) in specific settings; the channel effect is conditional on those media ecologies and
  need not generalize. Demographic significance must be assessed against the populations actually reached.
- **Redundancy risk with A.3, and why it is low.** Because A.20 shares "diffusion" and its seminal set
  with A.3, the frame will overlap A.3's heavily in raw counts. The stage-3 probe must show the *identified*
  overlap is thin — that A.20's identified core (the media-reach and network-position designs) is the set
  A.3 deliberately excluded as content-agnostic, not a re-run of A.3's fertility-control-knowledge cell.
  A.3's Wall-1 resolution is the prior that this will hold; the probe is the test.
