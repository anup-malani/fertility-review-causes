## Causal claim

The smartphone and app economy sharply lowered the price — in money, effort, and waiting — of
non-reproductive leisure: social media, streaming video, gaming, and pornography are now cheap,
instant, and engineered to hold attention. In a Becker household-production frame, fertility falls
either when the relative price of children rises or when an attractive substitute for the time and
commitment children require becomes cheaper. This hypothesis is the second case. Adults who would
otherwise spend social energy, time, and attention on courtship, partnership, cohabitation, and
eventually childrearing divert some of it to digital consumption. The substitution runs on two
budgets at once — **time** and **attention** — and is registered with three candidate
sub-mechanisms that share one upstream cause (cheap, absorbing digital leisure):

1. **Social displacement** — less in-person interaction → less couple formation;
2. **Pornography substitution** — a substitute for partnered sex → lower coital frequency with a partner;
3. **Motivational/leisure displacement** — absorbing leisure crowds out the orientation toward long-term relational commitment.

The mechanism is **economic** (a relative-price/substitution effect), not cultural: it holds the
value placed on children fixed and moves behaviour through the falling price of the outside option.
The value shift that *valorizes* self-oriented leisure is D.1.a and is treated there.

## This chapter's parameter (the §4 chain)

C.2.h owns exactly one parameter: **the effect on fertility (and its proximate partnering/coital
mediators) of a fall in the price or a rise in the absorptiveness of digital leisure**, net of the
other channels that move fertility over the same period. Concretely, the estimate this chapter seeks
is the effect on childbearing, union formation, or coital frequency of an *exogenous change in the
availability, cost, or engagement-intensity of digital leisure* (a network-technology rollout, a
broadband or smartphone diffusion shock, a change in data price or content supply), holding fixed:

- **the price of the mother's time / labor-market opportunity cost** — that substitution channel is **C.2.e**;
- **the matching technology of the dating market** — app-mediated couple formation is **A.24**;
- **coital frequency as a proximate biological determinant** — the frequency→fecundability→births identity is **A.14** (C.2.h owns only the *digital-leisure cause* of a frequency change, not the determinant itself);
- **the underlying value shift** toward self-actualization and self-oriented leisure — that is **D.1.a**;
- **clinically-mediated affective pathways** — where the effect runs through diagnosed anxiety/depression it is **D.3.a** (C.2.h owns only the *direct* time/attention displacement).

The falling price of the leisure substitute → this chapter. Why leisure became culturally valued →
D.1.a. The device used to *match* partners → A.24. The biological frequency identity → A.14. The
clinical distress pathway → D.3.a.

## Boundary walls (the load-bearing routing rules)

C.2.h shares a device (the smartphone) with A.24, a Becker substitution structure with C.2.e, a
proximate outcome with A.14, a cultural backdrop with D.1.a, and a hypothesized downstream effect
(anxiety) with D.3.a. Routing is the central screening task; the discriminators are stated at the
level of the *identified parameter*, not the keyword.

**Wall 1 — C.2.h vs C.2.e (Female Wage and Opportunity Cost of Time, `female-wage-opportunity-cost`). LOAD-BEARING.**
Both are substitution effects in the same Becker framework, and both can be described as raising the
"opportunity cost" of a child. The line is *which price moves*. C.2.e is a rise in the price of the
mother's **time** through the **labor market** — higher wages make the hours a child requires more
costly. C.2.h is a fall in the price of the **leisure substitute** — cheaper, more absorbing
non-child consumption makes the child's time-and-attention cost bind harder without any wage change.
Discriminator: a study identifying off wages, female labor-force participation, or labor-market
returns routes to **C.2.e**; a study identifying off the price, availability, or engagement-intensity
of digital leisure (a 3G/broadband rollout, smartphone diffusion, data cost, content supply) stays
here. A study that cannot separate a leisure-price shock from a coincident wage change has not
identified this chapter's parameter.

**Wall 2 — C.2.h vs A.24 (Dating Apps and Union-Formation Friction, `dating-apps-union-formation-friction`).**
Same device, different function. A.24 is the effect of app-mediated **matching** on couple formation
— dating apps changing match quality, search frictions, or the marriage market's clearing. C.2.h is
the effect of digital leisure **crowding out** the time and in-person interaction from which
partnerships form, independent of any matching technology. Discriminator: a study identifying off
dating-app adoption or matching-market mechanics routes to **A.24**; a study identifying off general
digital-leisure time displacement of face-to-face socializing stays here. Sub-mechanism (1) (social
displacement → less couple formation) is the near wall: a **bridge cell** exists where reduced
in-person socializing is the estimand — that is C.2.h even though the downstream outcome (fewer
unions) is also A.24's currency; the routing key is whether the paper's variation is in *matching
technology* (A.24) or in *leisure time crowd-out* (C.2.h).

**Wall 3 — C.2.h vs A.14 (Coital Frequency and Fecundability, `coital-frequency-biological`).**
A.14 owns coital frequency as a proximate biological determinant — the frequency → monthly
fecundability → births identity, regardless of what changes frequency. C.2.h's sub-mechanism (2)
posits that pornography (and absorbing leisure generally) *lowers* partnered coital frequency.
Discriminator: a study whose estimand is frequency → fecundability → births (the determinant itself)
routes to **A.14**; a study whose estimand is digital leisure / pornography → coital frequency (or →
fertility) stays here, with coital frequency read as a *mediator* on C.2.h's causal path, not as the
parameter. A.14 is the identity; C.2.h is one cause feeding it.

**Wall 4 — C.2.h vs D.1.a (Postmaterialism, Individualism, Secularization, `postmaterialism-individualism-secularization`).**
D.1.a is the value shift that makes self-oriented leisure *desirable*. C.2.h is the price/technology
shock that makes leisure *cheap and absorbing*, holding tastes fixed. Discriminator: a study
identifying off a technology or price shock (network rollout, device diffusion, data cost) routes
here; a study identifying off an attitude/value shift (survey-measured individualism, secular values)
routes to **D.1.a**. The conceptual risk is that observed digital-leisure use is endogenous to values;
the design must isolate the price/availability shock from the taste change.

**Wall 5 — C.2.h vs D.3.a (Mental Health and Anxiety Epidemic, `mental-health-anxiety-epidemic`).**
The registry names C.2.h as a *hypothesized upstream cause* of rising anxiety, so the two are linked
by assumption. The wall is by *pathway*. C.2.h is the **direct** time/attention displacement of
partnering and childrearing. D.3.a is the **affective/clinical** pathway — diagnosed or subclinical
anxiety/depression impairing pair-bonding and reproductive intention. Discriminator: a study whose
estimated mechanism runs through a measured mental-health state (a depression scale, a diagnosis)
routes to **D.3.a**; a study whose mechanism is direct behavioural crowd-out (time use, motivation)
stays here. A study that measures screen time → anxiety → fertility is a D.3.a mediation study; screen
time → fertility with no affective mediator is C.2.h.

No wall is declared unenforceable in advance. The A.24 social-displacement bridge (Wall 2) is the one
most likely to need a full-text call.

## Eligibility rules

- Include an empirical study only when its estimate bears on **digital-leisure price/availability →
  fertility or its proximate partnering/coital mediators**, holding the mother's wage/opportunity
  cost and the underlying value shift fixed or adjusted.
- A study identifying off wages/FLFP (C.2.e), dating-app matching (A.24), the coital-frequency
  determinant itself (A.14), the value shift (D.1.a), or a clinically-mediated affective pathway
  (D.3.a) routes to that hypothesis, even where it mentions screens or leisure in passing.
- SDT only. The exposure does not exist before the consumer internet / smartphone era, so evidence is
  contemporary (broadly post-2000, concentrated post-2010). Carry no PM or FDT evidence; there is no
  historical cell to fill.
- On every included empirical paper, record the **source of exogenous variation** (technology
  rollout, device diffusion, price/content shock, or none), which **sub-mechanism** it speaks to
  (social displacement / pornography / motivational), and what it holds fixed — the identification
  value is set entirely by whether the design separates a leisure-price shock from the reverse channel
  (low-fertility people consume more leisure) and from coincident SDT drivers (wages, values,
  housing) moving at the same time.

## Expected shape of the evidence (a caution, not a result)

- **The literature is thin and very recent.** Expect a small identified core dominated by a single
  design family — device/network-diffusion instruments for digital-leisure exposure (the archetype is
  Myers–Hooper's use of AT&T's 2007–2011 exclusive iPhone-carrier monopoly, with counties classified
  treated/control by AT&T wireless-broadband coverage) — surrounded by a larger body of correlational
  time-use and cross-country descriptive work. A thin core is expected and is a result to report, not
  a search failure.
- **The identification threats are reverse causality and common-trend confounding.** People with
  fewer children and no partner have more discretionary time and consume more digital leisure, so a
  raw correlation is biased toward the hypothesis; and smartphones diffused over exactly the years
  wages, housing costs, and postponement all intensified, so a national time series cannot separate
  the leisure channel from every other SDT driver. Credit only designs with plausibly-exogenous
  variation in leisure price/availability (a rollout discontinuity, a staggered-diffusion instrument).
- **The headline attribution is a strong claim on one design.** Myers–Hooper attribute 33–52% of the
  2007–2022 US TFR decline to smartphone diffusion. That is a large share resting on one instrument;
  the chapter's demographic-significance work is to scrutinize whether the identified micro effect can
  bear that macro attribution, not to inherit it.
- **The three sub-mechanisms may warrant separate GRADE cells** (per the registry note) if the
  evidence bases diverge — the pornography strand (Ryu) and the technology-rollout strand
  (Myers–Hooper) have different designs and different walls.

**Anchor verified 2026-09-21:** the primary seed is Myers, Caitlin K., and Ezekiel Hooper, "Is the
iPhone Birth Control? Causal Evidence from AT&T's 2007–2011 Carrier Monopoly," NBER Working Paper
w35310 (2026) — confirmed at nber.org/papers/w35310 and SSRN 6897299. Design: AT&T held an exclusive
iPhone carrier arrangement until 2011; counties are classified treated (>90% AT&T wireless-broadband
coverage) vs. control (<10%), and the iPhone's diffusion explains 33–52% of the decline in the
general fertility rate among women 15–44, with corroborating national time-use evidence of reduced
in-person interaction, increased pornography use, and lower sexual frequency (all three registered
sub-mechanisms). The registry's "3G carrier rollout" gloss is imprecise — the instrument is AT&T's
device-exclusivity monopoly, not a bare 3G rollout — but the mechanism it identifies is exactly
C.2.h's. Prefer a version of record on freeze if one appears; the working paper is the current VoR.
