# Search scope — sex ratio imbalance and marriage market effects

**Hypothesis:** A.10 (HYPOTHESES-v5.md)
**Hypothesis slug:** `sex-ratio-marriage-market`
**Target phenomena:** all three — PM, FDT, SDT. Identification quality is concentrated in FDT and SDT;
PM evidence is expected to be thin and largely non-causal (see "Expected shape of the evidence").
**Status:** DRAFT (Shravan, 2026-07-31) — walls and sign convention proposed, **not yet frozen.**
Freeze requires a second read on Wall 1 and a PI answer on the war-shock gap (Wall 5). Gold anchors
not yet sourced.

## Causal claim

Exogenous variation in the number of potential partners of the opposite sex, per person, in a
marriage-relevant population changes the probability that a person partners at all, the age at which
they do, and their bargaining position inside the partnership — and thereby changes fertility. The
distinguishing feature of A.10 among its neighbors is that the treatment is a **headcount**: how many
candidate partners exist, not who they are, what they earn, what children cost, or what anyone wants.
Fertility moves without any change in preferences, prices, or the technology of birth control.

A.10 is a **proximate** cause in the review's two-tier scheme. It does not explain why the ratio is
skewed; war, sex-selective abortion, differential mortality, migration, and incarceration do, and
those live elsewhere (Wall 3, Wall 5). The chapter takes the skew as given and asks what the marriage
market does with it.

### The treatment variable is not the sex ratio at birth

Three different ratios travel under one name, and the distinction is load-bearing for both the search
and the chapter's demographic-significance verdict:

- **SRB — sex ratio at birth.** Biologically ~105 boys per 100 girls, and close to that in most of
  the world most of the time. Skewed by sex-selective abortion in specific settings.
- **ASR — adult sex ratio** in the marriage-relevant age band. Moves with differential adult
  mortality (male excess mortality is large and near-universal), war deaths, sex-selective migration,
  and incarceration. Can depart from 100 substantially with SRB at exactly its natural value.
- **The operational or marriage-squeeze ratio** — men aged *x + δ* per 100 women aged *x*, where δ is
  the prevailing spousal age gap. Because women typically marry men a few years older, and because a
  growing population has larger younger cohorts, positive growth plus a positive age gap
  **mechanically produces a shortage of older men** — a squeeze generated by growth and nuptiality
  convention alone, with a perfectly natural SRB and no war, migration, or sex selection whatsoever.
  The squeeze reverses when growth turns negative.

**A.10's treatment is the operational ratio (or the ASR as its proxy), never the SRB.** A paper whose
independent variable is the SRB with no marriage-market-relevant construction is measuring the
determinant, not the treatment, and routes to `TREATMENT_DETERMINANT`.

## The standing PI prior (recorded as a prediction to test, not an assumption)

Anup's marginal note on A.10 in HYPOTHESES-v2.md, carried into the v5 `notes` field:

> I think this is a proximate cause because it raises the question of why the sex ratio imbalance
> occurred. Sometimes it's culture, sometimes it's war. As far as I can tell, nobody thinks it has had
> a first-order effect on total fertility, because globally sex ratios tend to be close to the
> so-called natural rate. Tell me if I'm wrong.

This is a demographic-significance prediction stated before the evidence, which is the right way to
have it, and §7 of the chapter should test it rather than inherit it. Two observations frame the test.

**The premise and the treatment are different statistics.** "Globally sex ratios tend to be close to
the natural rate" is true of the SRB. It is not what the marriage market clears on. The operational
ratio departs from 100 through the growth-plus-age-gap squeeze, through male excess mortality, and
through migration — all of which were *largest during the FDT*, when population growth rates were
high, mass transatlantic migration was sex-selective, and two world wars removed men from specific
cohorts of specific countries. So the premise, as stated, does not establish the conclusion. Whether
the effect is nonetheless second-order is an empirical question the chapter answers; the aggregate
constancy of the SRB is not evidence for it.

**But two arguments still push toward the PI's conclusion, and the chapter must weigh them.** First,
the classic **aggregation offset**: a skew that lowers one sex's fertility raises the scarce sex's
matching probability, so population-level births may move far less than either side's per-capita
rate. Total births are bounded by the number of women, and a marriage-market reshuffle mostly
reallocates births across men. Second, the skews that are large are **local and transient** — a
country-decade, a region, a birth cohort — and averaging over the world and over a century is exactly
the operation that makes them vanish. Both arguments are about the *aggregation*, not the mechanism,
which means A.10 could carry a well-identified, sizable local effect and a negligible global one at
the same time. That is a coherent verdict and may well be the chapter's.

Falsification, stated in advance: the prior survives if per-woman fertility effects at plausible
observed ratio departures are small, or if credible estimates concentrate in settings whose
population weight is trivial. It fails if the FDT-era squeeze or the post-2000 Asian cohorts move
per-woman fertility by a share of the observed decline comparable to what other chapters claim.

## The boundary walls (the load-bearing routing rules)

A.10 has a worse boundary problem than any hypothesis piloted so far, and the reason is a defect in
the hypothesis list itself: **C.7.a's claim text in HYPOTHESES-v5.md names "sex ratios" as one of its
own mechanisms** while cross-referencing A.10. As the two entries are written, a sex-ratio paper
satisfies both. Routing is therefore the central screening task, and Wall 1 is where a mistake costs
the most. (Recommended edit to the master list is noted at the end of this section; it is not made
here because HYPOTHESES-v5.md is under PI review at TICK-001.)

**Wall 1 — A.10 vs C.7.a (`marriage-market-economics`): headcount vs match quality.** The sharpest
and most consequential distinction.

- **C.7.a asks:** *given the people available, why do matches form less often, later, or worse?* Its
  mechanism is the quality and cost of matching — educational assortative sorting, search frictions,
  the collapse in the economic standing of available partners, marriage as a production complement to
  childrearing.
- **A.10 asks:** *what happens when there are simply not enough of one sex to go around?* Its
  mechanism is a numeric shortage.
- **Discriminator (operational):** *would the treatment still vary if every person in the market were
  identical to every other except for sex?* If yes, it is A.10. If the variation is in the
  **characteristics** of the available partners, it is C.7.a — even when the author expresses it as a
  ratio.
- **The "marriageable men" trap, stated explicitly because it will be the most common error.** An
  index of men-per-woman that conditions on employment, earnings, incarceration status, or education
  is a **quality-weighted ratio** and routes to **C.7.a**. The Wilson-style marriageable-pool
  construction and the trade-shock / manufacturing-decline literature both work this way: what moves
  is the economic standing of men, and the ratio is the accounting device. A **raw headcount** ratio
  routes to A.10. Where a paper reports both, the estimate identified off the raw ratio is A.10's and
  the quality-weighted one is C.7.a's; the paper appears in both chapters at the level of the
  estimate, never the study.
- Incarceration is the genuinely hard case and is decided by construction, not by topic: male
  incarceration removes men from the market by headcount (A.10) *and* selects on their economic
  quality (C.7.a). Extract which variation the estimate uses; if the design cannot separate them,
  tag it `MIXED_HEADCOUNT_QUALITY` and report it in both chapters as unallocated.

**Wall 2 — A.10 vs A.7 (`marriage-timing-age-at-marriage`): the mediator wall.**

- **A.7 owns** marriage timing and never-married shares → fertility, through exposure.
- **A.10 owns** sex ratio → fertility, **including the part that runs through marriage timing**,
  because A.10 owns the treatment and A.7 owns the mediator.
- **Discriminator:** does the estimate exploit variation in the *ratio*? If yes it is A.10's reduced
  form, whatever the mediator. If the paper estimates marriage timing → fertility with no ratio
  variation, it is A.7's.
- **Double-counting hazard — flagged for cross-chapter synthesis, not resolvable inside this
  chapter.** A.10's demographic significance and A.7's are **not additive**: most of A.10's effect
  travels down A.7's channel, so a review that sums per-hypothesis contributions will count the same
  births twice. The same hazard recurs for every proximate cause that runs through another proximate
  cause (A.10 → A.7, and A.10 → A.8 for parity stopping). The chapter must state its shared path
  explicitly in §7 and must not claim a contribution net of A.7 that it has not estimated. **This
  needs a review-wide accounting rule and is escalated with the Wall 5 item below.**
- A paper estimating sex ratio → marriage rate or age at marriage with **no fertility outcome** is
  mechanism/context (`MARRIAGE_ONLY_MECHANISM`), not an effect estimate. This will be a large class.

**Wall 3 — A.10 vs D.2.c (`son-preference-cultural`): generational timing.**

- **D.2.c owns** son preference → the **parents' own** fertility: continued childbearing until a son
  arrives, and the substitution of sex-selective abortion for that stopping behavior.
- **A.10 owns** what happens when the resulting skewed cohort **reaches marriageable age**, roughly
  20–25 years later, and its own fertility responds to the shortage of brides.
- **Discriminator:** *whose fertility is on the left-hand side, and in which generation?* The same
  cultural cause produces two estimands separated by a generation, and they are not the same effect.
- Sex-selective abortion → SRB, with no fertility outcome downstream, is a **determinant** of A.10's
  treatment: `TREATMENT_DETERMINANT`, context only.

**Wall 4 — A.10 vs A.9 (`population-age-structure-momentum`): mechanical composition.**

- A cohort with fewer women produces fewer **aggregate births** even when per-woman fertility is
  completely unchanged. That is accounting, and A.9 owns accounting effects.
- **A.10's primary estimand is a rate, not a count.** Aggregate-birth or crude-birth-rate outcomes are
  admissible only where the design holds cohort composition fixed or decomposes the mechanical from
  the behavioral component. Otherwise: `MECHANICAL_COMPOSITION`, route to A.9.
- This wall is why the sign convention below fixes a denominator (see "whose fertility").

**Wall 5 — the war-shock gap. An escalation, not a wall we can close.**

War mortality is the workhorse identification strategy for A.10 — it removes men from specific
cohorts of specific regions and is plausibly unrelated to their fertility preferences. But war also
destroys income and capital, widows women, traumatizes survivors, and is followed by demobilization
catch-up booms. **The exclusion restriction is the weak point of nearly every credible A.10 design:
the instrument moves much more than the ratio.**

- A war-based estimate is A.10-eligible **only if the design isolates the ratio channel** — typically
  cross-region variation in military mortality with the ratio as the stated treatment, ideally with
  the direct channels addressed.
- A paper estimating war → fertility **not through the ratio** has **no home hypothesis anywhere in
  this review.** HYPOTHESES.md's own merge notes flag this: "War, conscription, and military
  mobilization as a direct fertility shock (vs. only via sex ratios) — not enumerated despite a large
  historical-demography literature." Such papers get `OFF_WAR_DIRECT` and are **held in a queue with
  no destination.**
- **Escalate to Anup** (per RA-PLAYBOOK escalation rule: "a hypothesis does not fit cleanly into one
  category"). Two questions, one email: (1) does war/mortality-shock-as-direct-fertility-shock get
  enumerated as its own hypothesis, or does A.10 absorb it and change its own definition? (2) what is
  the review-wide rule for non-additive proximate causes (Wall 2)? Both block the freeze of this
  scope; neither blocks anchor sourcing.
- The same structure applies to famine and epidemic mortality shocks, which skew ratios and move
  fertility through several other channels at once.

construction. Flagged, not made — HYPOTHESES-v5.md is under PI review.)*

## Sign convention (pre-registered — the analogue of TICK-026 for OAS)

A pooled A.10 estimate is meaningless without this, because the sign is not fixed by the hypothesis.
Three independent axes determine it.

**Axis 1 — which sex is scarce.** A male-scarce market (post-war Europe, post-Soviet Russia) and a
female-scarce market (post-2000 China and northwest India) are not mirror images: marriage is not
symmetric in age, remarriage, or non-marital childbearing.

**Axis 2 — whose fertility.** Per-woman, per-man, per-couple, and aggregate births move differently
by construction. In a female-scarce cohort, per-man fertility falls sharply (surplus men never marry)
while per-woman fertility may *rise* (every woman matches, and matches earlier).

**Axis 3 — which channel.** These push in opposite directions, and separating them is the chapter's
value-added:
- **Exposure/matching:** the *abundant* sex loses. Its members fail to match, match later, or match
  worse, and their per-capita fertility falls.
- **Bargaining:** the *scarce* sex gains power inside the match (the Guttentag–Secord prediction; in
  modern collective-model terms, the sex ratio as a distribution factor). If the sexes differ in
  desired fertility, marital fertility shifts toward the scarce sex's preference — which can move
  fertility **up** in exactly the setting where exposure moves it **down**.

The conventions:

1. **Treatment direction.** Orient every extracted effect as the change in the outcome per **+1 unit
   increase in men per 100 women** in the marriage-relevant population. One unambiguous direction,
   whichever side of 100 the setting sits on. **Record the sample's baseline ratio on every effect**,
   because effects are not assumed linear or symmetric across 100.
2. **No pooling across the scarcity direction.** Male-scarce and female-scarce settings are pooled
   **separately**. Combining them assumes a symmetry the theory does not deliver. This is stricter
   than the OAS conservative pooling rule and is deliberate.
3. **Denominator.** The **primary estimand is per-woman fertility of the affected cohort** (TFR,
   children ever born, completed parity), because it is the review's standard outcome and the only
   one comparable across chapters. **Per-man and per-couple estimates are extracted and pooled
   separately, never combined with per-woman estimates.** Aggregate-birth counts are
   `MECHANICAL_COMPOSITION` unless composition is held fixed (Wall 4).
4. **Channel identification.** An estimate **conditional on being married** isolates the bargaining
   channel. An **unconditional** estimate is exposure plus bargaining together. Both are in the
   primary synthesis; they are reported as a pair and never pooled with each other. Where a paper
   reports both, the pair is the single most informative object the chapter can extract.

## Estimand cells

| Cell | Treatment / variation | Fertility outcome | Routing |
|---|---|---|---|
| `PRIMARY_EXPOSURE_UNCONDITIONAL` | Exogenous variation in the marriage-relevant sex ratio | Per-woman fertility of the affected cohort, unconditional on marital status | Primary synthesis |
| `PRIMARY_BARGAINING_CONDITIONAL` | Same | Marital fertility, conditional on being married | Primary / bridge — the value-added cell (isolates channel) |
| `PRIMARY_MALE_FERTILITY` | Same | Per-man fertility, childlessness, or completed parity of men | Primary, pooled separately (denominator differs) |
| `MIXED_HEADCOUNT_QUALITY` | Ratio construction that cannot separate headcount from partner quality (e.g. incarceration, mortality selecting on status) | Fertility | Primary, flagged unallocated; also reported to C.7.a |
| `MECHANICAL_COMPOSITION` | Ratio | Aggregate births / CBR with no composition adjustment | Route to A.9 |
| `MARRIAGE_ONLY_MECHANISM` | Ratio | Marriage rate, age at marriage, never-married share — **no fertility outcome** | Mechanism / context; cross-ref A.7 |
| `TREATMENT_DETERMINANT` | What causes the skew (sex selection, war deaths, migration, differential mortality) | No fertility outcome | Context; the son-preference part routes to D.2.c |
| `MARRIAGE_MARKET_THEORY` | Marriage-market theory, collective/bargaining models, formal marriage-squeeze demography | No empirical fertility estimate | Theory stream |
| `OFF_MATCH_QUALITY_C7a` | Quality-weighted partner availability, assortative mating, search frictions | Fertility | Route to C.7.a |
| `OFF_TIMING_A7` | Marriage timing / never-married share as the treatment, **no ratio variation** | Fertility | Route to A.7 |
| `OFF_SON_PREFERENCE_D2c` | Son preference → the **parents' own** fertility (try-until-a-son stopping) | Fertility | Route to D.2.c (and A.8) |
| `OFF_WAR_DIRECT` | War / famine / epidemic mortality shock → fertility **not through the ratio** | Fertility | **No routing target exists — hold and escalate (Wall 5)** |
| `OFF_OUTCOME` | Sex ratio → some other non-fertility outcome (savings, crime, labor supply, migration, violence) | No fertility outcome | Mechanism / context only |
| `OFF_OTHER` | Non-A.10 fertility determinant with no sibling-hypothesis home | Fertility | Route out; no sibling queue |
| `REVERSE` | Fertility behavior → the sex ratio (parity-progression plus sex selection producing the skew) | Sex-ratio outcome | Context, not the effect sought |
| `INSUFFICIENT_INFO` | Cannot be routed on the visible record | Unknown | Pairs only with `UNCERTAIN` |

`OFF_OUTCOME` is expected to be the **largest** cell by a wide margin. The sex-ratio literature in
economics is mostly about savings, crime, violence, labor supply, and female bargaining outcomes, and
only incidentally about fertility. That is a fact about the field and belongs in the chapter, not a
search failure.

## Eligibility rules

- Include an empirical study only when the estimate bears on **marriage-relevant sex ratio →
  fertility rate**. Sex-ratio papers with non-fertility outcomes seed mechanism and context; they do
  not enter the empirical recall denominator.
- The treatment must be the **operational or adult** sex ratio, or a defensible proxy for it. A
  raw SRB regressor with no marriage-market construction is a determinant, not a treatment.
- **Every effect carries:** the baseline ratio in the sample, the scarcity direction, the denominator
  (per-woman / per-man / per-couple / aggregate), whether it conditions on marital status, and the
  ratio's unit and construction (age band, spousal age gap δ, geographic market definition). Without
  these, no effect can be oriented and it cannot be pooled.
- **The market definition is itself a research decision and must be recorded**: a ratio computed over
  a country, a province, a marriage market defined by ethnicity or caste, or a birth cohort are
  different treatments with different plausible exogeneity. Expect this to be a major source of
  heterogeneity.
- A quality-weighted partner-availability index routes to C.7.a even when the paper calls it a sex
  ratio (Wall 1).
- Marriage-market theory and formal marriage-squeeze demography seed the **theory** stream and do
  **not** count toward empirical recall.
- All three phenomena are in scope; do not narrow. But **record the period and setting on every
  effect**, since the PM/FDT/SDT split is where the demographic-significance verdict is made and the
  three eras have very different squeeze magnitudes.
- Keep theory and off-cell papers discoverable but outside the empirical primary-cell recall
  denominator.

## When to adjudicate mechanisms

The title/abstract screen decides only whether a paper belongs in the empirical sex-ratio→fertility
stream, the theory stream, or an adjacent/off-cell stream. It does **not** require the RA to
determine the ratio's construction or the channel from an abstract.

**A caution carried over from D.3.b, where it cost us a pool.** The D.3.b stratum-A gate found that
the decisive Wall 1 failures were *invisible to any title/abstract screen* — the measure content only
became visible at full text. A.10 has the identical structure: whether a "sex ratio" is a raw
headcount or a quality-weighted index (Wall 1), and whether an estimate conditions on marital status
(the channel), are **full-text-only facts in most papers**. Plan for a bleed-in rate at the RA gate
and budget for it, rather than treating the screen's routing as final.

Full-text extraction must record, for every included empirical paper:

- `RATIO_CONSTRUCTION` — headcount / quality-weighted / mixed; the age band and spousal age gap; the
  geographic or social definition of the market;
- `SCARCITY_DIRECTION` — male-scarce or female-scarce, with the sample baseline ratio;
- `DENOMINATOR` — per-woman / per-man / per-couple / aggregate;
- `CONDITIONS_ON_MARRIAGE` — yes/no (this is what separates the two channels);
- `SOURCE_OF_VARIATION` — war mortality, sex selection, migration, incarceration, differential
  mortality, growth-plus-age-gap squeeze, or cross-sectional variation only;
- `EXCLUSION_THREATS_ADDRESSED` — which of the co-moving channels (income, widowhood, trauma, capital
  destruction, migration selection) the design actually handles.

Drafting may report only mechanisms supported by these fields. A cross-sectional correlation between
a regional sex ratio and regional fertility, with no source of exogenous variation, may document an
association but must not be described as evidence that the ratio *caused* the fertility difference —
regional sex ratios are driven by selective migration, which is correlated with everything.

## Expected shape of the evidence (a caution, not a result)

A.10 is the **opposite** of D.3.b: a well-studied hypothesis with a real quantitative economics
literature, credible natural experiments, and decades of formal demography. That changes what to
expect, and three of the four expectations are still cautions.

1. **This is the right hypothesis to exercise the cold-start bootstrap's channel 1** (prior
   meta-analyses and systematic-review included-study lists), which was thin on D.3.b and is
   GACS §7 move 5's replication test. But expect the reviews that exist to be about sex ratios →
   **marriage, labor supply, savings, or violence**, not → fertility. Those seed query vocabulary
   (leakage wall permitting) but not empirical anchors.
2. **Expect the OAS 44→10 collapse, worse.** The topical pool will be large and the estimand-ready
   pool small, because most of this literature's outcome is not fertility (`OFF_OUTCOME`), and much of
   what remains is `MARRIAGE_ONLY_MECHANISM` — the ratio's effect on marriage, with fertility left
   implicit. Predict the collapse now so it is not misread later as a search failure.
3. **Identification is unusually good and the exclusion restriction is unusually weak, at the same
   time.** War mortality, migration quotas, policy-induced sex selection, and convict transportation
   are genuinely exogenous to individual fertility preferences and genuinely move more than the sex
   ratio. Expect the risk-of-bias pass to concentrate on exclusion violations rather than on
   selection or measurement, and consider whether the standard domain set needs an
   exclusion-restriction domain added, as D.3.b added common-method bias (TICK-050).
4. **Expect attention skew toward post-2000 male-surplus Asian settings and toward the two world
   wars**, with pre-modern evidence thin and mostly descriptive. Since the demographic-significance
   verdict is made per phenomenon, a PM cell resting on almost nothing is a likely and reportable
   outcome.

## Cold-start channels and leakage wall

1. Prior meta-analyses and systematic-review included-study lists on sex ratios and fertility →
   empirical anchors by external authority. *(Leakage wall: a review's search strings may feed query
   terms and its included studies may feed anchors, but never the same study to both.)*
2. Top-down theory/canon enumeration — marriage-market theory, collective/bargaining models, formal
   marriage-squeeze demography — seeds the theory set and classic empirical anchors. Does not count
   toward empirical recall.
3. Citation snowball from the channel-1 and channel-2 seeds → the orthogonal Tier-B frame.
4. Broad single-query search plus a structured screen, **only if** the gold is still under the
   cross-validation floor (≥ 30 empirical anchors). Tier B is never drawn from this channel.
5. Production-query terms are not mined from a paper and then evaluated on it; learned extensions are
   fold-local once the gold frame exists.

## Pre-query anchor audit (not yet built)

The verified anchor set will be stored in `sex-ratio-marriage-market-cold-start-anchors.json`. Every
anchor must clear the **mandatory existence-verification gate** — a live DOI, or a Crossref/publisher
record confirming the title exists — before it enters any recall denominator. **No anchor is
hand-asserted from memory.** This is the standing rule from the 2026-07-08 run that found ~40% of the
frozen OAS Tier B was fabricated snowball citations.

A specific hazard for this hypothesis: A.10's literature has a small set of extremely famous papers
that an LLM will produce fluently and confidently from memory, in the exact register of a real
citation. That is precisely the failure mode the existence gate exists to catch, and the seminal
names carried in the HYPOTHESES-v5 entry are **candidates to verify, not anchors**. Because so much
of the surrounding literature has non-fertility outcomes, the anchor set must also deliberately carry
**off-cell decoys** — sex-ratio→savings, sex-ratio→crime, quality-weighted marriageable-men (C.7.a),
son-preference→own-fertility (D.2.c), and war→fertility-not-via-ratio — so the eventual query is
tested on routing as well as on topical retrieval. Routing, not retrieval, is this chapter's binding
constraint.
