## Stage 10 — demographic significance

No share is computable: the repository holds no fertility panel, so every cell is **NOT ASSESSED on the
arithmetic**, following C.2.c's precedent. But the prior question is answerable, and it settles more
than a share would.

**Slope sufficiency.** Over the SDT window the chapter's own exposure **expanded** — private credit to
GDP rose from **0.39 to 1.14** in high-income countries and **0.13 to 0.31** in LDCs, figures from
Filoso and Papagni's own text. And every identified estimate says expansion **raises** fertility:
+5.4% (US banking deregulation), +6 pp (US mortgage-market deregulation), +9.5 pp on a 7.7 pp base
(Bartik credit shock), plus one verified null.

> **A mechanism whose exposure moved in the direction that raises fertility cannot explain a fertility
> decline.** For the SDT, Arm B's identified evidence runs *against* the phenomenon. At most it is an
> **offset** — the SDT would have been larger without credit expansion — not a cause of it.

No share should be computed for that cell, because the sign forecloses the question. And the composite
cell is **contested at the sign**: Filoso and Papagni say +3.7 to +5% for developed countries, Suriani
et al. say negative and significant in all eleven models. Both unidentified; Suriani's Sargan test is
rejected throughout.

PM and FDT are **NOT ASSESSED** — in scope per the registry and empty of read evidence.

**Arm B / SDT is Moderate** on four identified designs consistent in sign, with one verified first
stage — downgraded once for **indirectness**, because the estimates answer *"does credit access raise
fertility"* while the chapter asks whether credit constraints explain the SDT decline. Not downgraded
for inconsistency: the null sits on the intensive cost margin and the positives on the extensive access
margin, which is a coherent pattern rather than a contradiction.

**Empty cells take "No evidence", never Very low** — Very low asserts a poorly identified literature
where there is none.

## The result, stated plainly

**C.3.e's best-identified evidence does not support C.3.e as an explanation of fertility decline.** It
supports the opposite: credit access raises fertility, credit access expanded, and so the mechanism
worked against the SDT rather than producing it. The registry's PM/FDT configuration — children as
savings vehicles where finance is absent — has **no read evidence at all**, and the arm that carries it
lost three of its five read records at full text.

## §6 — why this is not a chapter draft

**22% of the primary pool is read, and where screen cells have been tested against their own texts they
have failed about two-thirds of the time.** Twelve have been overturned, eleven of them by removal. The
arm counts (B 14, composite 40, S 11) are an upper bound.

Three specific things would move the verdict:

1. **Arm S retrieval.** Nine unread records, and its GRADE is No evidence in two of three phenomena.
2. **The two weak-venue identified studies** — Thailand's Million Baht Village Fund (negative,
   middle-income) and China's bank-competition deregulation (positive). Both are unrefereed or
   near-unrefereed and both bear directly on the sign question.
3. **Anup's ruling on Wall 1**, which is applied as an RA decision and determines whether Arm S exists.

And stage 11 requires **three independent raters**. This is one. A single rater arguing both sides is
not a panel, and the GRADE table above should be read as a draft for adjudication.
