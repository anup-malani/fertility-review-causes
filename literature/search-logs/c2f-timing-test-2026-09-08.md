## What this does and does not license

**It bounds the mechanism's share before any elasticity is applied.** In the median country only
about **31%** of the SDT decline remains after the period in which the exposure was moving the wrong
way. That is an upper bound on what C.2.f could explain even if its elasticity were large and
perfectly identified, and it is derived from the exposure and the outcome alone.

**It is not uniform, and the exceptions are real.** IRL (31%) and USA (34%) had most of their decline
*after* their inequality trough, which runs with the hypothesis. GRC never turned up at all (post-
trough slope -0.075), so its "trough" is only the window minimum and its 84% should not be read as a
U-shape result.

**Three limits, stated rather than buried.**

1. **TFR is a period measure and the 1965-1980 collapse is partly tempo, not quantum.** Postponement
   depresses period TFR without an equivalent fall in completed cohort fertility -- that is A.11's
   chapter. A cohort series would put less of the decline in the pre-trough window. The direction of
   this bias is against the finding, so the 69% is an overstatement of unknown size, and the test
   should be re-run on completed cohort fertility (HFD) before the number enters a verdict.
2. **The trough of measured top-share inequality is not necessarily when the mechanism switches on.**
   The registered claim is explicitly about *rising* inequality, so the test is fair to the claim as
   written, but a version of the hypothesis running on the level rather than the change would not be
   tested by this.
3. **11 of 21 countries are excluded for thin pre-1980 coverage**, and they include GBR, DEU, ITA,
   ESP and JPN. That is a coverage limit, not a finding.
