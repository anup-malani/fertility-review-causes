# D1 deterministic rank and screening cutoff — twinning-multiple-births (A.12)

**Frame in: 8,701 Tier B records.** 359 version duplicates collapsed on normalized title (most-cited kept), leaving **8,342 scored records**.

**Worklist out: 1,376** — 800 from the budget slice (SCREEN_BUDGET=800), plus 40 orthogonal-channel, 212 Wall 8, and 324 both-axes bypasses. **6,966 records go unread by the semantic screen** and their character is reported below, because a bounded screen that goes unstated reads as a complete one.

Score at the budget margin: **36**. A D1 score is a queue position, not a verdict; no record is deleted and every one keeps its score, rank and hit lists, so the cutoff can be re-cut later without re-running retrieval.

## The two axes are anti-correlated here, which is why neither is up-weighted

Every previous chapter had one near-universal axis (the exposure) and one scarce axis (the outcome), and up-weighted the scarce one. A.12 cannot do that. The A4 diagnostics show the twin-IV half of the frame carrying fertility vocabulary WITHOUT twinning vocabulary (Black-Devereux-Salvanes: twin 6.0%, fert 32.1%) and the demography half carrying twinning without a population fertility quantity (Pison & D'Addato: twin 84.0%, fert 47.0%). Up-weighting either axis would systematically demote one half of the chapter, so the two carry equal weight and the **cross-axis AND** does the discriminating.

**Records carrying both axes: 952 of 8,342 (11.4%).** 952 are in the worklist (**100.0%** — the both-axes bypass exists to make this 100%).

**Records with the twin-IV signature (twin term AND design vocabulary): 57.** 57 are in the worklist (100.0%).

## Wall 8, measured

Of **1,991 records reached from a twin-IV canon seed, only 154 mention a twinning term at all (7.7%).** That is Wall 8 stated as a number rather than as a claim in the scope document: a twin-IV paper's title and abstract are about schooling, earnings or labour supply, and the twin birth appears only in the first-stage table. **92% of this chapter's identification neighbourhood is invisible to axis 1.**

The first version of the Wall 8 bypass required a twinning term as well as the design vocabulary and recovered 4 records — the condition was self-defeating, not the population small. It now gates on seed provenance plus 2 independent design terms and no twinning term, because requiring one re-imposes precisely the visibility assumption the wall denies. 293 first-stage-seeded records carry the design vocabulary; 404 of the 1,991 are in the worklist (20.3%).

The bypass admits family-size-IV papers GENERALLY, not twin-IV papers specifically — the instrument is often unnamed in an abstract too, so the admitted set includes one-child-policy and sibling-sex-composition designs beside twin designs. That is deliberate. D1 is a queue, not a verdict; separating them is the semantic screen's job and, for the first-stage table itself, full text's. Admitting a same-shaped design that turns out not to use twins costs one screen read; excluding it costs a record no later stage can recover.

## What the cutoff drops, and its character

Of 6,966 unread records: **2,064 depend on a homonym seed alone** (29.6%), 3,263 on a routing decoy alone, and 210 carry a non-human term. The homonym share is the intended effect: A4 COUNTED that cloud's on-topic rate rather than sampling it — SHELX 13 on-topic of 87,673 citing works, TWIP steel 0 of 1,810, both 0.0% — so demoting it costs the chapter nothing measurable. Nothing is deleted, and `seed_ids` provenance survives on every record.

## Budget note

SCREEN_BUDGET is set to 800 and the worklist came out at 1,376 because the bypasses add 576 records the cutoff would have dropped. That is the intended shape: the budget governs the ranked head, and the bypasses govern completeness on the three populations where a miss is unrecoverable — the orthogonal channel, the Wall 8 first stages, and every both-axes record. If the semantic screen's real capacity differs, change SCREEN_BUDGET and re-run; the bypasses should not be traded away for budget, because each one protects a population that cannot be recovered downstream.
