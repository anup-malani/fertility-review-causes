## The gate cell, read

Three distinct studies (Comolli 2020/2021 are one study in two versions), and **they disagree**:

- **Behrman and Taubman 1989** is the only study that measures Easterlin's benchmark the way
  Easterlin specified it — **income observed for two generations**. Parents' income enters at
  **−0.032 (t = 1.5)**, grandparents' at −1.0 (0.7) and 0.92 (0.7); own income is **not positively
  significant**; R² = 0.04. The authors' own verdict is that the test **favours the Becker
  formulation**. N = 1,463 couples from the NAS-NRC twin registry plus an offspring survey, with a
  Heckman correction for subsample selection.
- **Comolli 2021** (PSID 2003–2017, Cox hazard, 2,709 men / 2,956 women) finds the opposite sign:
  holding an occupational position as prestigious as one's parents' raises the first-birth hazard,
  **HR 1.17\*\*\* to 1.27\*\*\***. The effect **attenuates to non-significance** in the
  younger-cohort and working-only subsamples.
- **Elder 1981** measures Depression scarcity in the household of origin but reports no
  relative-income effect estimate; it is a two-cohort life-course comparison, not a test.

So the cell that was supposed to carry the chapter's identification claim contains **one direct test
that rejects the hypothesis in favour of Becker, and one indirect test that supports it.** Neither
is identified. That is a finding, and it is the opposite of a thin cell being uninformative.

## A sign trap that would have corrupted the pooled estimate

**Pampel 1993 defines relative cohort size as prime-age-over-young** — the *inverse* of the
young-over-prime ratio this chapter's own sign test uses (script 306). His **+1.87\*\*\*** is
therefore *consistent* with Easterlin's negative prediction. Pooling it against a young/prime-defined
coefficient without re-orienting would flip the sign of the pooled estimate. Recorded in the row and
flagged for the pooling rule; **every remaining `COHORT_SIZE_FERTILITY` extraction must record the
direction of its ratio explicitly.**

## Two independent confirmations of the scope §5 sign test

Neither was sought; both fell out of extraction.

- **Jeon and Shields 2005** report, in their own summary statistics for 23 OECD countries, TFR
  falling **2.04 → 1.62** across 1975–1999 while their age-structure variable falls **0.607 → 0.421**.
  Exposure and outcome move together, which is the wrong direction for the hypothesis — the same
  co-movement script 306 measured independently from World Bank data. They add that the predicted
  post-bust recovery "is much smaller than earlier studies predicted."
- **Suzuki 2019** finds a **structural break in Japanese fertility determinants at 1975–1980**
  (Chow F = 64.61, p < 0.001) — the same hinge at which the sign test stops working.

## The rival cell is genuinely contested

Butz and Ward's own 1979 paper adds **proxies for Easterlin's relative-economic-status variable** to
its specification, so the founding horse race is in the founding paper. Macunovich (1993, 1996) runs
it the other way and argues the female wage's effect is *conditional* on male relative income.
Diebolt and Doliger 2005 run it explicitly and **decline to pick a winner**. Coskun and Dalgic 2022
argue fertility has become *procyclical*, which fits neither model.
